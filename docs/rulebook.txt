The Void Throne Playtest

Player’s Rulebook

Five players / 60 minutes

The conquest has ended, but the ships, soldiers and institutions who made it possible still need a future. You have two months to show that your proposed government can work, then decide who will hold power when the emergency is over.

1. Introduction

The Void Throne is a game of negotiation about governing a new interstellar empire. You will talk, make offers, write decisions into law… and then live with their consequences.

There are five player roles:

Two from Ductus Guild, which controls navigation and transport.

Two from the Janissariat, the army that won the war.

The Ascendant, the ruler who united the empire and can see the future.

You face one connected emergency: A space passage called the Narrow is deteriorating. The star system of Sorenne's Fall needs supplies, and a cohort of Janissariat needs transport, but your ships and forces cannot do every job.

At the same time, you are writing two parts of the empire's constitution: who can restrict transport between the stars, and who can restrict military operations.

2. The Settled Dark

Roughly two hundred inhabited worlds, stations and habitats form the Settled Dark. For three thousand years, six great dynastic Houses ruled it through the Conclave of Thrones, but over decades of conquest, a figure known only as the Ascendant broke their power.

The new empire is called the Settled Dominion and its rulers are negotiating a Charter of Settlement: the constitution that will define the institutions of the new order. The negotiations take place at the Seam - the ancient space station the Ascendant rules from.

The Ductus Guild

Interstellar travel depends on Guild navigators, who learn to sense and interpret the immaterial deep through which ships travel. Owning a ship is not enough: you need their specialist expertise. The Ductus Guild uses that dependence to defend its licences, professional independence and commercial obligations.

The Guild's Navigation Rights Petition, called GUILD-14, has waited fourteen years for a formal answer in the Charter of Settlement.

The Janissariat

The Janissariat is an enormous army of psychically conditioned warriors. That conditioning made them an effective instrument of war. It frays without fighting, weakening combat power and the habits that sustain imperial military authority.

That gives the army a difficult incentive: a peaceful month may help the Dominion while weakening the Janissariat in the future.

The Ascendant

Little is known of the Ascendant, but that they appeared leading the Janissariat at the start of the conquest, and that they have the power of foresight.

The Ascendant begins with imperial authority: the power to veto an order for the following round. The factions retain control of their assets and can carry out any unvetoed orders they can supply.

The Crisis

The Narrow is a strategic passage between dense stellar formations, linking the inner systems to the southern rim. Supplies passing through it sustain Sorenne's Fall, a star system of forty million people living beneath the debris of a battle fought before Pale Meridian.

Sorenne's Fall governs itself through the administration left behind when House Sorenne fell. The Sorenne Flag is the surviving fleet of the fallen house. It scattered rather than surrender at Pale Meridian and continues to resist the Dominion. It also delivers irregular supplies to Sorenne's Fall: destroying its ships would leave the system harder to govern.

Spear, a Janissariat cohort, is held at the Narrow while its commanders seek passage to the Flag's anchorage beyond it.

3. Players and councils

The Guild and the Janissariat each have two players. Each pair shares assets and objectives, but sit on different councils.

The Ascendant is a single player, supported by the army and able to spend their own power on foresight.

All three parties need to reach a settlement.

4. Sequence of play

The game consists of two rounds, each representing a month. A round has five minutes of faction phase, a five minute council phase and a five minute free phase.

After the second round, there will be a ten-minute Ceremony of Settlement to resolve the new government.

Faction Phase — five minutes

You have five minutes to decide your faction's approach with your teammate. Guild and Janissariat players stay at their own faction tables and do not speak or send messages to the other faction during this phase. The Ascendant may visit either faction.

Your assets are the ships or military units your faction controls. Each has a card showing its owner, council and traits. Traits describe what the asset can do; orders require traits rather than a particular named asset.

Keep an asset at your faction table for its home action, or take its card to the council printed on it. An asset at a council is offered for service there; its home action does not happen that round.

Agree the placement of your cards with your teammate. Spear and Grey Petrel have two different home faces: turn the card to show the home action you want. Their traits are the same on both faces. At a council, either face makes the asset available for orders.

You may move or turn cards until the faction phase ends. Then take the agreed cards to their councils and leave the others at home, with the chosen home action showing. This arrangement records your decisions; there is no faction decision sheet. Cards cannot change tables or turn to a home action during the council phase.

In round two, a Standing Commitment may already fix where a card goes or which home action it shows. This is a promise agreed and recorded during the previous free discussion. Put a commitment marker on the card and follow that promise.

If the teammates cannot agree their card arrangement before the deadline, leave that faction’s cards at home on these default actions. Binding Standing Commitments override defaults:

Guild: Wayfarer [Fulfil Commercial Contract]; Lantern [Maintain the Narrows].

Janissariat: Spear [Stand Down]; Vigil [Defend Army Bases].

Remove lost or disabled cards from play. They cannot supply an order or perform a home action.

Council Phase— five minutes

Go to your council and discuss the orders you can carry out using the traits of assets brought to the table. Stay at that table throughout the council phase and speak only with its members. Do not visit, call across to or send messages to the other council, including through your faction teammate. The Component Reference explains available orders, their requirements, purpose and risks; the facilitator keeps exact calculations concealed. Make cross-council plans during earlier faction visits or free discussion. Those plans do not bind later decisions.

You may select any combination of orders for which you have the assets and requirements. Each asset and each printed order can be used once per round. Check the public vetoes: a struck-out order cannot be chosen this round. GN records Navigation allocations, including Grey Petrel when offered; JS records Security allocations. The other council members contribute through discussion. Authority does not add a requirement for their approval.

At the end of the phase, confirm the chosen orders and the asset used for each. Resolve any double use before handing in the council sheet. An asset with an unclear allocation does neither disputed job; valid orders still proceed. The facilitator collects both programmes before revealing either. The Ascendant remains at Security and Government.

Free Phase— five minutes

After round one, discuss the results and record next-round vetoes under the existing articles. Each entitled party may strike out one order in each council where it has a veto. Joint authority gives a separate veto to each party that offered an available asset to either council this round; the Component Reference explains who exercises it. Choosing None leaves the options open. Vetoes are public, last one round and remain in force if the article changes.

Then discuss the government you would like to try. Propose one Navigation article and one Military article. The two council representatives concerned and the Ascendant must agree an article aloud; the facilitator records the result. The articles are decided separately. Where no agreement is reached, the existing arrangement continues.

You may attach a Standing Commitment to a proposed article to fix one of your faction's asset assignments for round two. Both teammates must agree that promise. Record the asset, assignment and article together; the Component Reference gives the permitted commitments and their conditions. Articles and attached commitments that pass govern round two.

After round two, use this discussion to negotiate the final settlement package. There is no further operation or recovery before the Ceremony, and no next-round veto to issue.

By the end of the phase the facilitator resolves both councils, home actions and scenario conditions, then announces track changes, asset losses, completed contracts and token expenditure.

5. The Ceremony of Settlement

After round two, check each party's voting power and begin the ten-minute Ceremony. You are voting on a complete package: one Navigation article and one Military article. They need not be the articles tried provisionally.

Voting Power

Guild: 1 for an intact Wayfarer, 1 for surviving Lantern navigators, and 1 for every 2 points of Beacon.

Janissariat: final Readiness.

Ascendant: final Readiness plus unspent foresight.

Voting

On the final settlement sheet, tick one box beside each player's role: Assent or Do not assent. Both Guild players must assent for the Guild's weight to count; both Janissariat players must assent for the army's weight to count. The Ascendant assents individually. An unticked row or disagreement within a faction gives that party no supporting weight. No handwritten signatures are needed.

Add all available weight, including parties that refuse or abstain. The package needs at least two-thirds of that total, rounded up, and signatures from at least two parties with positive weight. A total weight of zero cannot ratify a settlement.

Example: Guild 4, Janissariat 3 and Ascendant 4 give eleven in total. Two-thirds rounds up to eight. Guild and Ascendant can pass a package with eight; Guild and Janissariat have only seven. If the Guild players split, their faction contributes no support, but its four stays in the total.

There is no automatic veto. A package can therefore pass over a party's refusal and impose duties on that party.

Two ballots

If the first package fails, negotiate a replacement before the deadline. Collect fresh votes whenever either article changes; previous approvals do not carry over. You may hold up to two ballots. Stop when a package passes.

If no package passes, both provisional articles expire and both councils return to Interim. Interim is the fallback, not an option on the final ballot.

The physical result remains: lost assets stay lost, tracks stay where they finished and spent foresight does not return. Ratification settles the articles, but the people governing Sorenne's Fall will have to live with what those decisions have done.

6. Debrief

What government did you want at the start, and what did you accept at the end? Name a decision or event that changed your view. What did your faction give up, what did it receive, and what did your teammate contribute?

For the Ascendant, did foresight change a decision, a bargain or only your confidence? One remaining token is inevitable after two purchases. Declining a purchase is the choice that preserves more power.
