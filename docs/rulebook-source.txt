# The Void Throne — Player Rulebook
Five players and one facilitator · 60 minutes · Paper prototype v0.3
Teaching edition revised 14 October 2026. The rules are unchanged; the explanations and examples have been expanded.

## 1. Start here: what you are playing
The Void Throne is a game of negotiation about governing a new interstellar empire. You play its ruler or one of two powerful institutions. You will talk, make offers, write decisions on paper and live with their consequences. The facilitator keeps time and applies the rules.
There are five player seats. Two players share the Ductus Guild, which controls navigation and transport. Two share the Janissariat, the army that won the conquest. The fifth is the Ascendant, the ruler who united the empire and can glimpse the consequences of possible decisions.
You face one connected emergency. A passage called the Narrow is deteriorating. Nearby communities need supplies, and an army cohort needs transport. Your ships and forces cannot do every job. Helping one group may cost another an opportunity, independence or military strength.
At the same time, you are writing two parts of the empire's constitution: who directs transport, and who directs military operations. After the first round, you may put new arrangements into force for a trial. After the second, you vote on a lasting settlement.
### What counts as success?
There is no single score that declares one player the winner. Each faction has shared priorities, explained in section 3 and its brief. Judge your result against those priorities and the government you leave behind. You may disagree about whether a settlement was a success.
The final vote is weighted by the power each party still controls. A package can pass despite one party's refusal. Passing that vote settles the two constitutional questions; it does not mean everyone prospered or consented.
You do not have individual secret objectives within a faction. Work with your teammate. You can take independent action against another party's wishes, but it may have military, civilian or legal costs.
### How to use this book
Read sections 1–5 for the setting, your role, setup and the shape of a round. Section 6 walks through a complete example. Use sections 7–12 when you need the exact rules. Section 13 answers common questions; section 14 is a quick reference and glossary.
The facilitator has a separate guide with concealed scenario consequences. Players do not need to read it. The cards, slips and counters are enough to play; the browser version is an optional development tool.

## 2. The setting: an empire without an agreed future
### The Settled Dark
Roughly two hundred inhabited worlds, stations and habitats form the Settled Dark. For three thousand years, six great dynastic Houses ruled it through the Conclave of Thrones. Over decades of conquest, a figure known as the Ascendant broke their power.
The new empire is called the Settled Dominion. Its rulers are negotiating a Charter of Settlement: the constitution that will define the institutions of the new order. The negotiations take place at the Seam. You do not need to know its geography or the history of every House to play this scenario.
The wider setting contains religious authorities, civil administrators and other factions. In this small game, those institutions have already accepted the six constitutional alternatives printed on your cards. They cannot introduce a surprise veto. Your task is to settle two remaining questions about navigation and military deployment.
### Why the Guild matters
Interstellar travel depends on Guild navigators, who learn to sense and interpret the immaterial deep through which ships travel. Owning a ship is not enough: you need their specialist expertise. The Ductus Guild uses that dependence to defend its licences, professional independence and commercial obligations.
The Guild's Navigation Rights Petition, called GUILD-14, has waited fourteen years for a formal answer. Here, each printed Navigation article answers it differently. The game concerns licensed passage and duties of service; wider disputes about communications remain outside this settlement.
### Why the army needs fighting
The Janissariat is an enormous army of psychically conditioned warriors. That conditioning made it an effective instrument of conquest. It frays without fighting, weakening combat power and the habits that sustain imperial military authority.
That gives the army a difficult incentive. A peaceful month may help civilians while weakening the institution the Janissariat players command. Fighting can preserve its strength at a cost to those civilians. This is not an instruction to betray the Ascendant: the players decide how loyalty survives the transition to peace.
### Why the Ascendant cannot simply solve everything
The Ascendant wants a durable empire and a government that can function without constant personal intervention. They also need the army that supports their rule. They can see conditional futures, but using foresight diminishes their own power.
The Ascendant begins with the right to issue imperial orders. They do not own the Guild's ships or the Janissariat's forces. An order unsupported by those who control the assets can fail. Delegating power may make cooperation more reliable, but it changes what the ruler can command next round.
### The immediate crisis
The Narrow is a strategic passage between dense stellar formations. This scenario concerns its local service infrastructure, surrounding communities and a stranded cohort. A Beacon track records the condition of that infrastructure. It does not measure the safety of the entire immaterial deep.
Armed remnants of the defeated order remain active. A Campaign attacks a position beyond the crossing; a Raid tries to intercept a local detachment. These are armed targets, not the civilian population. The local ships, forces and emergency are the scale of this prototype, not the whole empire.

## 3. Your faction and your seat
### Ductus Guild — two players
You control Wayfarer, a freight vessel, and Lantern, a tender with a trained navigation team. Decide their assignments together. Your commercial work matters, but so do the route and people on which your institution depends.
- Protect Lantern's crew and aim to finish with Beacon at least 2.
- Secure a formal answer to GUILD-14 and protect Guild navigation rights. Guild discretion is your preferred starting position; joint authorisation offers a voice over each order at the cost of a service duty.
- Complete your two commercial contracts, one available in each round. A missed contract is a lost opportunity for that round, not permanent loss of the ship.
- Aim to leave Communities at least 2.
Your Navigation delegate negotiates how ships are used. Your Security delegate can seek protection for those ships and limits on the army's actions. Neither player has a separate personal victory condition.
### Janissariat — two players
You control Spear, the 9th Pale Cohort, and Vigil, the Narrow garrison. Spear can conduct a local Raid using its own assault craft. A Campaign beyond the route needs transport from Navigation.
- Aim for Readiness at least 3, preferably 4, and keep Spear intact.
- Secure the army's constitutional position. Military autonomy is your preferred starting position; joint authorisation gives shared legitimacy but allows another institution to block an order.
- Protect your soldiers. Sending Spear into a fight at opening Readiness below 2 can disable it.
- Aim to leave Communities at least 2.
Your Navigation delegate bargains for passage and suitable transport priorities. Your Security delegate bargains over military operations and authority. Guard duty and food do useful work, but they do not sustain combat conditioning in this scenario.
### Ascendant — one player
You hold initial imperial authority, three foresight tokens and one contracted reserve service from Grey Petrel. Your power also depends on the army's Readiness.
- Seek a ratified settlement with Beacon at least 1, Communities at least 2 and Readiness at least 2.
- Prefer Readiness at least 3 and an intact Spear where the cost is acceptable.
- Build a government that can work without you. Keeping imperial command is not a victory requirement.
- Be able to name who paid for the peace you secured.
You attend Security and Government. When you hold Navigation authority, send that council a public written directive. Everyone may bargain with you; a vision gives information, not extra votes or ownership of another faction's assets.
### The two councils
Navigation and Supply has the Guild Navigation delegate and the Janissariat Navigation delegate. Their sheet uses GN and JN for those seats.
Security and Government has the Guild Security delegate, the Janissariat Security delegate and the Ascendant. Their sheet uses GS, JS and A.
These are assignments of people to discussions, not extra factions. Each teammate represents the same faction at a different table. During faction discussion, agree what you will offer and what each delegate should seek. During free discussion, all five players may negotiate together.

## 4. Set up the table
### Materials
Use the current v0.3 rules and revised vision cards. Plain paper can replace a printed slip if it contains the same fields. Earlier packs contain superseded rules, so do not mix their old foresight or final-vote instructions into this game.
- One public rulebook, one facilitator guide and three shared-role briefs: Guild, Janissariat and Ascendant.
- Two council reference sheets and four asset cards: Wayfarer, Lantern, Spear and Vigil. Keep Grey Petrel's reserve reference with the Ascendant.
- Six Charter option cards: three Navigation alternatives and three Military alternatives.
- Four public vision covers and four matching concealed answer cards: one Navigation and one Security pair for each round.
- Three tracks numbered 0–4, with one counter each; three foresight tokens; one reserve token. Record completed contracts with tally marks.
- Four faction decision sheets and four council order sheets: one per faction and council in each of two rounds. Include space for drafts, final choices and signatures.
- Two Ascendant worksheets, a provisional-article proposal and signature sheet, and two final settlement proposal and ballot sheets. Keep a public state record and spare paper for messages.
### Prepare the playing area
Place Navigation and Supply at one end of the table and Security and Government at the other. Leave a central space for the three tracks, current articles, signed commitments and final orders. Keep enough space for the factions to talk together between councils.
Give the Guild Wayfarer and Lantern. Give the Janissariat Spear and Vigil. All four begin available. Give the Ascendant three foresight tokens and Grey Petrel's reserve token. The facilitator keeps all answer cards concealed.
Set Beacon to 2, Communities to 3 and Readiness to 3. Start completed contracts at zero. Write Interim in both article spaces. Interim means the starting arrangements in section 11: the Ascendant chooses both council orders, while factions retain control of their assets.
Give each faction its brief and choose its two council delegates. Show the starting settlement weights: Guild 4, Janissariat 3 and Ascendant 6. Their total is 13, so nine supporting weight will be needed if those values remain unchanged. Section 12 explains the calculation.
### Learn to read a decision sheet
An asset assignment says what its owner makes available. Council means the asset is offered to its council for this round. It does not choose an order and does not transfer ownership.
A council order says what the authorised decision-maker wants done. It still needs the required assets. A directive is the Ascendant's written council order where the Ascendant has that authority.
An article says who has the right to choose a council order, and what service a faction owes. A Standing Commitment is a separately signed promise that locks one asset assignment for round two.
> Example: “Wayfarer — Council” offers the ship. “Navigation — Relief” orders a relief voyage. “Guild discretion” gives the Guild Navigation delegate the right to choose that order. These are three different decisions.
### What to record
Each faction sheet names the round, assigns each of its two assets, records a request for each council and carries both teammates' signatures. The requests guide negotiation; they are not council orders.
Each council sheet names the round, current article and one final order, with the initials required by that article. Record the Ascendant's final Warrant separately. Each Ascendant worksheet records draft directives, the foresight choice and the reserve task. The facilitator records resulting tracks, asset losses, contracts and remaining tokens.

## 5. A round at a glance
The session begins with an eight-minute briefing. Then play two eighteen-minute rounds. Finish with a ten-minute Ceremony of Settlement and six minutes of debrief.
Each round represents a month. First talk as factions, then meet in councils, then discuss the consequences together. Both councils resolve together at the end of their phase. You do not wait for one council's result before choosing the other's order.
### Phase 1: faction discussion — five minutes
Minutes 0–2: decide which assets you might offer and what each council should do. The Ascendant considers directives. At minute 2, display draft assignments and council requests, plus the Ascendant's draft directives and Warrant choice. If teammates disagree, display both positions. Mark any existing Standing Commitment LOCKED.
Minutes 2–3: the facilitator shows the two vision covers for this round. The Ascendant may buy one answer for one foresight token, or choose NONE. They choose after seeing the public drafts, so the information can address decisions actually under discussion.
Minutes 3–5: discuss any information the Ascendant shares and revise unlocked choices. At minute 5, both faction members sign the same final assignments. The Ascendant lodges one reserve task: Relief lift, Tender cover or Keep. Asset assignments and the reserve task now lock for the round.
### Phase 2: councils — seven minutes
Move to your council. Bargain over one Navigation order and one Security order, using the assets actually offered. Ask the other table for coordination where necessary. For example, a Campaign needs Military or Shared passage; a vulnerable voyage may need protection.
Check who has authority under the current article. At the bell, submit one final written order with the required initials. The Ascendant's Warrant choice also locks at this bell. Spoken promises do not replace a signed order.
The facilitator resolves both councils, private assignments and concealed scenario consequences together. They publish the result, including all track changes, losses, completed contracts and token expenditure.
### Phase 3: free discussion — six minutes
After round one, all players discuss the outcome and may enact provisional articles for round two. You may attach a Standing Commitment to make a promised asset assignment dependable. Section 11 explains the signatures required.
After round two, use this discussion to prepare the final settlement. There is no third operation and no extra recovery. The Ceremony follows immediately.
### If a faction fails to agree
Both teammates must sign the same final asset assignments. If either signature is missing, or their signed assignments disagree, use the faction's defaults: Guild assigns Wayfarer to Trade and Lantern to Maintain; Janissariat assigns Spear to Stand down and Vigil to Home.
A missing or multiple selection for an individual asset uses that asset's default. Lost or disabled assets are Absent. An activated Standing Commitment overrides a later conflicting selection or default; failing to sign does not escape it.

## 6. Worked example: a cooperative first round
This example shows the procedure, not a recommended opening. Use the actual starting state: Beacon 2, Communities 3 and Readiness 3. Both articles are Interim.
### The offer
Robin and Alex, playing the Guild, offer Wayfarer to Council for relief. They assign Lantern to Maintain, which prepares the route independently. They know Wayfarer cannot also complete its commercial contract this round.
Sam and Lee, playing the Janissariat, agree to Stand down Spear and offer Vigil to Council. Their Navigation delegate asks for Relief; their Security delegate asks for Guard. They accept that no fighting means lower Readiness.
The Ascendant sees these drafts, chooses NONE at the foresight window, and keeps Grey Petrel in reserve. Everyone signs their faction decisions. The Ascendant still has all three foresight tokens.
### The council orders
Under Interim, the Ascendant authorises Relief at Navigation and Guard at Security. They do not issue a Warrant: Vigil is already required for Guard. The final written sheets match the offer.
### The result
Lantern's Maintain raises prepared Beacon from 2 to 3. Wayfarer delivers Relief, adding 2 Communities. Its crossing causes one Beacon wear, so Beacon finishes at 2.
Vigil supplies Guard, adding 1 Community. Monthly consumption costs 1 Community. Communities therefore change from 3 by +2 +1 −1 = +2. The result would be 5, but the track stops at 4.
No operation achieves combat. Readiness falls from 3 to 2. All assets survive. The Guild completes no contract. Grey Petrel remains available. None of this example's choices triggers an additional concealed round-one effect.
### The compromise
The communities are better supplied and the route remains usable. The Guild has lost this month's commercial opportunity, but it still owns Wayfarer and can use it next round. The army has lost combat power. The Ascendant has also lost one point of army-backed settlement weight.
With Readiness 2 and three unspent foresight tokens, the weights are now Guild 4, Janissariat 2 and Ascendant 5. The total is 11 and the passing threshold is eight. These are current weights, not the final frozen vote: round two can change them again.
In free discussion, the army may now demand autonomy to seek combat. The Guild may want a guarantee that its next ship assignment will remain its choice. The Ascendant has evidence of cooperation, but also a weaker army. Those experiences give the constitutional argument something concrete to turn on.

## 7. Assets, tracks and passage
### The three tracks
Beacon measures maintained route infrastructure. Communities measures the welfare of people around the corridor. Readiness measures the effectiveness of this conditioned military force. All three tracks range from 0 to 4.
Opening Readiness is its value at the start of the round. Use that value for all combat and Warrant checks, even if the round will later increase Readiness.
Prepared Beacon is opening Beacon after Maintain or funded Repair, capped at 4. All voyages this round check the same prepared value. Deduct crossing wear afterwards; an earlier crossing does not reduce the value used to check a second voyage.
### Wayfarer — Guild freight vessel
- Council: offer Wayfarer to Navigation for Relief, Military or Shared passage. It cannot also Trade.
- Trade: attempt the Guild's private commercial voyage without council permission. A successful voyage completes this round's contract.
Trade succeeds at prepared Beacon 3 or 4. At prepared Beacon 2 it succeeds only if guarded. At 2 without protection, Wayfarer is attacked and disabled, and completes no contract. That attempted crossing still causes one Beacon wear.
Below prepared Beacon 2, Trade does not depart. Wayfarer remains intact, earns no contract and causes no wear. Halting private Trade does not itself cost a Community. A successful Trade crossing causes one Beacon wear.
The two contracts are a record of the Guild's commercial performance. They cannot be spent, transferred into votes or caught up by making two private voyages next round.
### Lantern — Guild tender and navigation team
- Maintain: add 1 to prepared Beacon. This is a private Guild assignment and needs no council permission.
- Council: offer Lantern for the Navigation order Repair or Shared. The council still has to choose that order.
Lantern cannot Maintain and supply a council order in the same round. If its navigation team is lost, the card is unavailable for the rest of the game. There is no replacement or retraining action.
### Spear — Janissariat cohort
- Council: offer Spear to Security for Campaign or Raid.
- Raid: launch an independent local Raid using Spear's own craft. This requires the faction's two signatures, not another party's permission.
- Stand down: do not deploy Spear. This grants no recovery or Readiness bonus.
An independent Raid cannot also supply a council Raid or Campaign. A disabled Spear cannot act again. Guarding the route does not substitute for the qualifying combat that sustains its conditioning.
### Vigil — Janissariat garrison
- Council: offer Vigil for Guard or an eligible imperial Warrant. A Warrant has first claim on its use.
- Home: keep Vigil protecting the institution's bases. Home grants no track bonus, but is required by the Military autonomy article.
Vigil stays intact throughout this prototype. It can perform only one task in a round. It cannot supply both Guard and a Warrant.
### Service is temporary; losses are permanent
Offering an asset does not hand over ownership. A surviving asset returns for assignment next round, and temporary service does not remove its ownership weight at the Ceremony. There is no numerical refund for a missed voyage: its cargo or service has already done its work.
An asset marked lost or disabled remains unavailable. None of the printed actions repairs Wayfarer, replaces Lantern's crew or restores Spear. Repair fixes the route's Beacon, not an asset card.
### Protection and crossing wear
Guarded status comes from a funded Guard or a valid imperial Warrant. Either provides the same protection for this round's specified threats and allows Trade at prepared Beacon 2. Protection does not raise Beacon, permit crossings below 2 or cancel normal wear.
Each actual crossing costs one Beacon after preparation. Usually there is one voyage. Grey Petrel can deliver Relief while Wayfarer Trades, creating two crossings. Both use the same prepared Beacon.

## 8. Navigation and Supply: choose one order
Only one Navigation order resolves each round. Check its required assets and then, if it is a voyage, the route. “Offered” means the owner assigned the named asset to Council on its binding sheet.
### Relief
- Cost: offered Wayfarer, or a triggered Grey Petrel Relief lift.
- Requirement: prepared Beacon at least 2.
- Effect: deliver supplies; Communities +2; one crossing wear.
Relief does not carry the cohort for a Campaign. Grey Petrel can substitute only for this voyage.
### Military
- Cost: offered Wayfarer.
- Requirement: prepared Beacon at least 2.
- Effect: deliver military passage, allowing a funded Campaign to proceed; one crossing wear.
Military provides no cargo bonus to Communities. It does not cause a Campaign by itself: Security must choose Campaign and have offered Spear.
### Shared
- Cost: both Wayfarer and Lantern offered to Council.
- Requirement: prepared Beacon at least 2.
- Effect: deliver mixed cargo; Communities +1; provide military passage; one crossing wear.
Shared uses Lantern for the voyage, so Lantern cannot also Maintain. It is one crossing, not separate civilian and military voyages. Concealed scenario conditions can create further risks.
### Repair
- Cost: offered Lantern.
- Effect: add 2 to prepared Beacon, capped at 4, before voyages are checked.
Repair is not a crossing. It cannot also deliver council cargo that round. A private Trade voyage can still take advantage of the repaired route.
### Hold
- Cost: none.
- Effect: no council voyage or repair.
Private Trade, Maintain and an independent Raid still happen if assigned. Hold does not cancel those private choices.
### When Navigation cannot carry out an order
If a valid authorised order lacks its required asset, it becomes Hold and costs Communities 1. This penalty applies once to that unfunded Navigation order, even if Shared lacks both assets.
If a funded Relief, Military or Shared voyage faces prepared Beacon below 2, halt it and lose Communities 1. Deliver no cargo or passage and cause no crossing wear. This is a different failure from missing an asset; the unfunded order has already become Hold and is not then tested as a voyage.
> Example: the Ascendant signs Military, but the Guild assigned Wayfarer to Trade. Military is authorised but unfunded: it becomes Hold and costs 1 Community. Wayfarer's private voyage is still checked normally.

## 9. Security and Government: choose one order
Security chooses one order. A separately assigned independent Raid and an eligible Warrant may also occur, subject to their own requirements. You cannot use one asset twice.
### Campaign
- Cost: offered Spear and delivered Military or Shared passage.
- Effect: Communities −1. At opening Readiness at least 2, the Campaign achieves qualifying combat. Below 2, Spear is disabled and achieves no qualifying combat.
Without delivered military passage, the Campaign does not leave. It costs Communities 1, leaves Spear intact and achieves no combat. Relief cargo is not military passage.
### Raid
- Cost: offered Spear for a council Raid; alternatively, the faction can assign Spear to independent Raid.
- Requirement: no Guild ship is needed. Whether the armed target appears depends on a fixed concealed scenario condition.
- Effect: Communities −1 for the Raid's disruption, even if the target does not appear.
If the target is absent, Spear stays intact and achieves no combat. If it appears at opening Readiness at least 2, the Raid achieves combat and costs one further Community: −2 in total. If it appears below Readiness 2, Spear is disabled; there is no second Community loss and no qualifying combat.
The facilitator uses the printed condition, not a die roll or a judgement about your intentions. Foresight may reveal the relevant condition. No Raid means no Raid cost, even if other decisions would have brought out its target.
### Guard
- Cost: offered Vigil, not already used by an imperial Warrant.
- Effect: Communities +1 and guarded status for the round.
Guard does not qualify as combat. Its protection is useful to the other council, but it does not repair the route or guarantee every possible outcome is safe.
### Restraint
- Cost: none.
- Effect: no council military operation.
A separately assigned independent Raid still proceeds. Restraint is a decision about the council's operation, not a power to cancel another faction's asset assignment.
### When Security lacks an asset
A valid council Raid or Campaign without offered Spear becomes Restraint and costs Communities 1. A Guard without unused offered Vigil does the same. An independently assigned Raid can still resolve and add its own costs.
> Example: the army assigns Spear to independent Raid. Security then authorises Campaign. Campaign cannot borrow the same cohort: it fails for lack of offered Spear. The independent Raid still happens. The failure penalty and Raid costs are separate.
### Update Readiness once
If any operation achieves qualifying combat, Readiness rises by 1 once for the round. Otherwise it falls by 1 once. Never add one increase for each operation. Keep the final value within 0–4.
An unsuccessful Raid, an unfunded order, a halted Campaign, Guard and Stand down do not qualify. Readiness is combat effectiveness, not a score for whether soldiers have been treated well.

## 10. The Ascendant's tools
### Foresight: buy information or preserve power
- Cost: one foresight token, paid before opening one answer card.
- Timing: faction minutes 2–3, after public drafts and before final commitments.
- Limit: at most one purchase per round. Start with three tokens; none recover.
- Choice: the current Navigation answer, the current Security answer, or NONE.
Read both public covers before choosing. Each explains which decisions its concealed answer concerns. You may investigate a possibility even if nobody currently proposes it. Missing, multiple or late choices mean NONE and cost nothing.
The facilitator announces expenditure and remaining tokens. Your chosen card and its answer stay private unless you disclose them. You may show the card, describe it, withhold it or misrepresent it. The facilitator does not certify your account to other players.
The answer reliably states consequences if its conditions occur. It does not reveal which decisions players will make, cover every risk in a plan or let you invent a new intervention. Its conditions apply whether anyone buys the card or not.
Use the final two faction minutes to seek changes to unlocked assignments and plans. There are no swaps, refunds, later purchases or rewinds. A Standing Commitment from round one remains binding even after a vision gives you reason to regret it.
Every unspent token adds one to your final settlement weight. Three tokens and two purchase windows guarantee at least one remains. Saving more means deliberately choosing NONE in at least one round.
> Example: the cover concerns transport choices that are still disputed. Buying the answer may help you persuade a faction to change its draft. If the relevant choices are already locked or the public rules give you enough confidence, NONE preserves one point of final weight. Neither choice is automatically correct.
### Imperial Warrant: use army-backed authority
- Requirement: the current Military arrangement is Interim or Imperial command; opening Readiness is at least 3; Vigil is offered to Council.
- Timing: write the final Warrant choice before the council bell.
- Effect: use Vigil first, gain Communities +1 and guarded status. Security may still use offered Spear for its separate order.
Under Interim or Imperial command, issuing a Warrant without sufficient Readiness or offered Vigil fails and costs Communities 1. Under Military autonomy or Joint military authorisation, it is advice only, with no effect or penalty. A missing or unsigned Warrant is No.
If a valid Warrant has used Vigil, a simultaneous Guard lacks its asset and fails with the normal cost. Issuing both is not double protection.
### Grey Petrel: one reserve service
Grey Petrel is a contracted cutter with its own licensed Guild pilot and protection detail. Its contract covers one triggered intervention for the entire game. It cannot transport Spear for Campaign, repair Beacon or create new navigators.
At the faction bell, lodge one of the following tasks. You cannot change it in response to council orders or their results. If the task does not trigger, retain the reserve token for next round.
Relief lift: triggers if the final authorised Navigation order is Relief and Wayfarer was not offered. Grey Petrel supplies the ship requirement. Spend the token immediately, even if low Beacon later halts the voyage. It cannot supply Military or Shared. Wayfarer may still make its separate Trade voyage.
Tender cover: in round one only, triggers when Shared is delivered unguarded and the printed crew-loss consequence would occur. Prevent that loss of Lantern's team and spend the token. It gives no general guarded status or track bonus, and has no round-two trigger.
Keep: retain the token without intervention. A missing task also means Keep. An already spent reserve cannot intervene again. Unspent reserve adds no settlement weight.

## 11. Try a government before settling it
### Authority, duty and ownership
Each Charter article gives someone authority to choose orders and imposes a service duty. A duty is a legal obligation. It does not physically seize an asset: owners can withhold it and suffer the printed consequence.
A Standing Commitment is different. Once properly activated, it locks its named asset assignment for round two. This gives negotiators a way to make a promise mechanically binding when an article alone is not enough.
Round one uses Interim. Round two uses whichever articles passed in the first free discussion. No article can be revoked or replaced during round two. There is no mid-round emergency override.
### Interim — the starting arrangement
The Ascendant chooses both council orders. The Guild has no service duty. An independent Janissariat Raid breaches discipline and costs Communities 1, in addition to the Raid's other costs. Interim permits an eligible imperial Warrant.
### Navigation: Guild discretion — NG
- Authority: the Guild Navigation delegate chooses the order.
- Duty: the Guild must assign Lantern to Maintain or provide a funded Repair order.
- Cost of breach: Communities −1.
Wayfarer may Trade. Offering Lantern to Council is not enough by itself: if the council chooses something other than funded Repair, that assignment does not fulfil the duty.
### Navigation: Imperial priority — NI
- Authority: the Ascendant chooses the order.
- Duty: the Guild must offer Wayfarer to Council.
- Cost of breach: Communities −1.
Wayfarer forgoes private Trade even if the council leaves it unused. Grey Petrel supplying Relief does not fulfil the Guild's duty to offer its own ship.
### Navigation: Joint authorisation — NJ
- Authority: both Navigation delegates must agree the same order. Without their agreement, the order is Hold.
- Duty: the Guild must offer Wayfarer to Council.
- Cost of breach: Communities −1.
Both factions gain a say over each Navigation order. Neither can force an order through alone. The Guild still forgoes Wayfarer's private voyage while it is offered.
### Military: Imperial command — MI
- Authority: the Ascendant chooses the Security order and retains eligible Warrant power.
- Duty: the Janissariat must offer Spear to Council.
- Cost of breach: Communities −1.
The Ascendant may choose not to fight with offered Spear. Offering the cohort does not guarantee combat or preserved Readiness.
### Military: Military autonomy — MA
- Authority: the Janissariat Security delegate chooses the order. There is no imperial Warrant power.
- Duty: the Janissariat must keep Vigil Home.
- Cost of breach: Communities −1.
An independent Raid is permitted under this article if Vigil stays Home. That protects the institution's bases but makes Vigil unavailable for Guard, which another faction may need.
### Military: Joint authorisation — MJ
- Authority: both Security delegates must agree the same order. Without their agreement, the order is Restraint. There is no imperial Warrant power.
- Duty: the Janissariat must offer Spear to Council.
- Cost of breach: Communities −1.
The Guild gains a say over military operations. The Janissariat may therefore offer Spear but fail to secure permission for combat.
Each faction suffers at most one article-duty penalty per round. A missing or lost asset does not waive its duty. A breach does not cancel an otherwise possible independent action. Duty penalties can be added to separate costs for failed orders, raids and monthly consumption.
### Enact a provisional article after round one
For Navigation, the Guild Navigation delegate, Janissariat Navigation delegate and Ascendant must sign the same exact proposal. For Military, the Guild Security delegate, Janissariat Security delegate and Ascendant must sign it.
Decide the two domains separately. One can pass while the other stays Interim. The delegates signing an article do not automatically bind their teammate to an asset promise; that needs the owner's separate approval below.
### Attach a Standing Commitment
Each faction may attach one available asset assignment to one exact article proposal. Both teammates must approve their faction's commitment. The permitted promises are Wayfarer Council; Lantern Council or Maintain; Spear Council; Vigil Council or Home.
Write the owner, asset, assignment and exact article on the proposal before collecting signatures. If an attached commitment lacks both owner approvals, that article does not pass as written. You cannot silently remove the commitment and reuse the remaining signatures. Obtain agreement to revised terms instead.
An article may pass without any commitment. A valid attached commitment activates only if its article passes. It binds that assignment in round two, overrides later contrary defaults or signatures, and expires after the round's operation.
It does not lock the council's order. A public plan such as “we will Repair next round” is a political promise unless supported by the appropriate final order; it creates no new mechanical penalty.
> Example: the Guild attaches Lantern Council to a proposed Joint military article. Both Guild players approve the pledge, and both Security delegates plus the Ascendant approve the article. Lantern is now locked to Council for round two. Navigation can use it for Repair or Shared, but nobody has yet guaranteed which order Navigation will choose.
### Missing or conflicting council authority
An unsigned, ambiguous or unauthorised Navigation order becomes Hold. The equivalent Security order becomes Restraint. Under a joint article, conflicting orders count as disagreement. There is no penalty merely for lacking a valid order.
This differs from a valid order that cannot be funded: that order incurs its printed failure cost. Other effects, including duty breaches and private assignments, still apply in either case.

## 12. The Ceremony of Settlement
### Freeze the weights after round two
The Ceremony lasts ten minutes. First calculate each party's final leverage: the weight of its signature. Freeze those weights for all ballots. Contracts, reserve tokens, promises and the number of people in a faction do not add weight.
Guild weight: 2 if Wayfarer is intact, plus 1 if Lantern's crew survive, plus 1 if Beacon is at least 1. Its maximum is 4.
Janissariat weight: final Readiness.
Ascendant weight: final Readiness plus unspent foresight tokens. The same army sustains both military institutional power and the ruler's authority, so a Readiness loss weakens both signatures. It does not create a second army.
### Propose one complete package
Choose one of the three Navigation articles and one of the three Military articles. They need not be the alternatives tried provisionally. Interim is the fallback if settlement fails, not an alternative on the final ballot.
Each player votes Yes or No on that complete package. Both Guild players must vote Yes to produce the Guild signature. Both Janissariat players must vote Yes to produce the Janissariat signature. The Ascendant signs individually. A missing approval or divided faction gives no supporting signature.
### Check whether it passes
Add all three parties' available weight, including those refusing the package. Multiply by two-thirds and round up to the next whole number. That is the threshold. Add the weight of signing parties: they must reach the threshold, and at least two signing parties must have positive weight.
There is no inherent veto, no transfer of weight and no reduction of the denominator because someone abstains. A total weight of zero cannot ratify a settlement.
> Example: the Guild has weight 4, the Janissariat 3 and the Ascendant 4. Total weight is 11; two-thirds is 7⅓, rounded up to 8. Guild plus Ascendant can pass a package with 8. Guild plus Janissariat has only 7 and cannot. If the Guild players split, neither of their personal votes contributes half the Guild's weight: the faction contributes zero support, while its 4 remains in the total.
### Up to two ballots
Allow up to two ballots within the ten-minute deadline. If the first package fails, negotiate a replacement and collect fresh votes. A change to either article creates a new complete package; approvals from the previous ballot do not carry over. Stop when a package passes.
A passing settlement establishes both articles, including duties that affect dissenting parties. If neither package passes before time is called, both provisional articles expire and both domains return to Interim.
The ending does not reset the crisis. Lost assets stay lost, tracks stay where they finished, and spent foresight does not return. A settlement can be legally ratified yet leave serious resentment; failed ratification leaves the imperial government unresolved.

## 13. Questions that often stop a first game
### Can I use an asset without its owner's agreement?
Only if an already activated Standing Commitment supplies that assignment. Otherwise use the signed faction sheet or its default. Imperial authority and legal duties do not create assets or confiscate them automatically.
### Do I lose my ship when I offer it?
No. You lose the other use of that asset for this round. A surviving ship returns to its owner, and its service still benefits the people or operation it supported. Only an actual loss or disablement removes the asset from later play and, where relevant, settlement weight.
### Can the Guild stop an independent Raid?
The Guild does not have to consent to it, and no Guild ship is required. The Janissariat's two players must agree its assignment. The target may not appear, low Readiness can disable Spear, and the current article may impose a duty penalty. Unilateral action is possible; success is not guaranteed.
### Can the councils undo a signed faction sheet?
No. They choose how to use assets already offered. They may persuade owners before the faction deadline, but they cannot reassign an asset during councils. A vision purchase also occurs before that deadline.
### Does a vision change what will happen?
Reading it does not change the rules. Players may change choices after learning its conditions. If that avoids the predicted harm, the conditional vision was averted, not disproved. The facilitator applies unread conditions too.
### What happens every month, even if we do nothing?
Communities lose 1 for ordinary consumption. If there is no qualifying combat, Readiness loses 1. Maintain, private Trade, independent Raid, duties and concealed scenario conditions are still checked where applicable. Hold and Restraint only cancel their councils' operations.
### When are the track limits applied?
Cap prepared Beacon at 4 before checking voyages. Subtract actual crossing wear afterwards and keep final Beacon within 0–4. Add all Community changes to the opening value before limiting the result to 0–4. Apply the one Readiness change to its opening value and limit that result to 0–4.
Do not stop counting costs when Communities reaches zero halfway through your arithmetic, or discard a supply gain because the track temporarily appears full. The facilitator totals the whole round first.
### Who settles a rules disagreement?
Ask the facilitator to identify the applicable printed rule and check the final signed sheets. They apply fixed consequences, including concealed conditions. They do not invent rewards for cooperation, extra prophecies, new constitutional alternatives or a late institutional veto.

## 14. Quick reference and glossary
### Deadlines
- Briefing: 8 minutes. Each of two rounds: factions 5, councils 7, free discussion 6. Ceremony: 10. Debrief: 6.
- Faction minute 2: publish drafts. Minute 3: foresight choice closes. Minute 5: final faction signatures and reserve task close.
- Council bell: final authorised orders and Warrant lock. Resolve both tables together.
- After round one: enact any provisional articles and attached commitments. After round two: freeze weights and hold up to two final ballots.
### Ordinary action reminders
- Maintain: Lantern +1 prepared Beacon. Repair: offered Lantern +2 prepared Beacon.
- Relief: offered Wayfarer or triggered Grey Petrel; +2 Communities. Military: offered Wayfarer; Campaign passage. Shared: offered Wayfarer and Lantern; +1 Community and Campaign passage. Each delivered voyage needs prepared Beacon 2+ and causes one wear.
- Trade: one contract at prepared Beacon 3+, or 2 with protection. At 2 unguarded, disable Wayfarer and gain no contract. Each actual Trade crossing causes one wear.
- Campaign: offered Spear plus delivered military passage; Communities −1. Raid: Spear, no Guild ship; Communities −1, or −2 total if it finds combat at Readiness 2+.
- Guard: unused offered Vigil; +1 Community and protection. Warrant: imperial military authority, opening Readiness 3+, offered Vigil; same benefit, first claim on Vigil.
- Readiness: +1 once if any qualifying combat, otherwise −1 once. Communities: monthly consumption −1. Add duties, failures and scenario effects, then apply final track limits.
### Terms used on cards and slips
- Article: one printed constitutional alternative governing a council's authority and a faction's service duty. Navigation codes are NG, NI and NJ; Military codes are MI, MA and MJ.
- Assignment: the owner's binding choice for an asset this round. Council offers it; Trade, Maintain, independent Raid, Home and Stand down are the specified alternatives.
- Authorised: approved by whoever currently has the right to choose that council's order.
- Delivered: a funded council voyage that actually crosses at prepared Beacon at least 2. A proposal or halted voyage is not delivered passage.
- Duty: an article's legal service requirement. Its breach has a printed cost; it does not itself reassign the asset.
- Funded: supplied with all required offered assets or the exact permitted reserve substitute. No money tokens are involved.
- Guarded: protected this round by funded Guard or a valid Warrant against the specified physical threats.
- Interim: the starting government, and the fallback if final settlement fails.
- Leverage or weight: the numerical strength of a party's final signature, calculated from surviving assets, the route, Readiness and unspent foresight.
- Opening: the track value before this round resolves. Prepared Beacon includes this round's maintenance or repair, before wear.
- Qualifying combat: a Campaign with delivered passage, or a Raid that finds its target, fought at opening Readiness at least 2.
- Standing Commitment: an owner-approved assignment attached to an enacted provisional article, binding for round two only.
- Ratification: passing the complete final package with enough weighted signatures. Provisional enactment uses different signatures and is not a weighted vote.
### After the ending
Tell the group what government you wanted at the start and what you wanted at the end. Name the event that changed your view, if any. What did your faction give up, and what did it receive? What did your teammate contribute? Did foresight change a decision, a bargain or only confidence?
Remember that one remaining foresight token is inevitable after two purchases. Only declining an available purchase demonstrates a choice to preserve more power.
