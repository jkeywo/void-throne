The Void Throne — Lore and Adaptation Record

How to use this record

This document records the established setting, explicit author corrections and the five-player adaptation separately. Historical source files are preserved with their original paths and SHA-256 checksums in the repository’s lore/source-inventory.json. This document contains facilitator spoilers from the larger game.

[WORKING] The current prototype borrows the setting and its institutional tensions. It does not silently turn every larger-game subsystem or secret character objective into a rule for the small game.

Established setting: the Settled Dark

Roughly two hundred inhabited worlds, stations and habitats make up the Settled Dark. The six dynastic Houses of the Conclave of Thrones governed it for three millennia. The Ascendant broke their order over a conquest beginning sixty years ago; the last loyalist fleet broke at Pale Meridian eleven months before the original Charter Day. The source describes the Ascendant as ninety-six years old. Source: archive/legacy-lore/Setting.md.

The Charter negotiations take place at the Seam. The proposed empire is the Settled Dominion. The Charter is unfinished and contested; the original wider game includes the Houses, Administratum, Ecclesiatum and other institutions. The phrase “sixtieth year of the Settled Peace” sits beside the recently completed conquest in the source; preserve that phrasing as a chronology question rather than inventing a correction.

The Ductus Guild

Guild navigators make interstellar travel possible by sensing the immaterial deep. The Guild’s indispensability is its political leverage. Navigation Rights Petition GUILD-14 has waited fourteen years for a response. Licensing, discretion and the unresolved scope of “associated services” matter; the Guild is not canonically a civilian-first charity. Source: Factions/Ductus Guild.md and Game/The Charter of Settlement.md.

Facilitator-only larger-game lore: navigation is reception of a transmission, not merely innate talent. Its character has changed. Different Guild characters know different pieces of the change, unexplored routes and possible communications. This prototype omits that information puzzle and its individual secrets; it does not refute them.

The Janissariat

The Janissariat’s three billion psychically conditioned warriors made conquest possible. Conditioning is designed for war and frays in peacetime. The constitutional question is whether the army is a sacred autonomous order, an imperial instrument, or another institution subject to negotiated oversight. The original High Command has internal disagreements; this prototype replaces secret individual victory conditions with shared faction priorities. Source: Factions/Janissariat High Command.md.

Facilitator-only larger-game lore: conditioning derives from early Ductus research and suppresses a perceptual capability. Fraying can release that perception in people trained to treat it as a threat. It is not simply physical illness, moral decline or guaranteed peacefulness. Source: Lore/Fraying Conditioning Thread.md.

Explicit author clarification in this design conversation: not fighting costs combat power, creating an incentive for aggressive choices others may dislike. The Ascendant has their loyalty while they fight and needs a viable army while seeking a better future. In the prototype, qualifying combat sustains Readiness; peaceful rounds reduce it. This does not establish automatic treason after one quiet month.

The Ascendant and the Throne Eternal

The Ascendant wants an empire that deserves to exist and a settlement that avoids future succession wars. The source depicts Communion as a process of transformation and uncertainty about whether they can verify their own goodness. Older mechanics include Tarot, Clarity and conditional orders. Those are historical mechanics, not the present small-game rules. Source: The Ascendant.md.

The Throne Eternal is the Ecclesiatum’s name for something ancient and directional, not simply a conventional benevolent god. Facilitator-only records distinguish discovery from a managed religious interpretation; its destination is uncertain. Source: The Throne Eternal.md.

Explicit author clarification: using foresight diminishes the Ascendant’s power; unspent foresight should contribute to settlement leverage. The Ascendant need not remain in control if they can trust the final government to govern without them. The three-token budget and weighted signature formula are prototype implementations of this instruction, not claims about a universal magical exchange rate.

The Narrow and the prototype inventions

The Narrow is an established strategic corridor linking inner systems and the southern rim, with a waystation council that has governed for thirty generations. Sorenne's Fall in the Southern Houses Arm provides the civilian stakes for this prototype. Vethis Secundus remains a different setting location whose resistance is collective non-cooperation; it is not the armed opponent here. Sources: Systems/Vethis Cluster.md; Systems/Southern Houses Arm.md.

Established setting: Sorenne's Fall is a star system of forty million people living beneath a battle debris field. Its former colonial administration continued governing after orders stopped arriving. The Sorenne Flag scattered rather than surrender at Pale Meridian and has been bringing the system irregular supplies. Campaign Ash pursues House Sorenne's surviving fleet. Sources: Systems/Southern Houses Arm.md and Game/Fringe Campaigns.md.

Scenario adaptation: the current crisis makes the Narrow the supply passage serving Sorenne's Fall. Stability replaces the former Communities label and records supplies and functioning civil services on the same 0–4 scale, with the same starting value and numerical effects. The Flag anchorage beyond the crossing, local patrol, traffic-linked appearances, named assets and two-month timetable are scenario additions. No value on Stability implies political loyalty or turns the system's civilians into military targets.

Beacon measures service infrastructure. Guild work prepares it; military protection addresses named physical attacks and exposed berths. Guard does not repair a psychic relationship with the void. Grey Petrel already has a contracted licensed pilot; the Ascendant never conjures a navigator.

The small game settles two outstanding instruments within a wider Charter. Nonplayed institutions are assumed to have accepted all six permitted alternatives and to provide formalities including sanctification. This removes a surprise veto without rewriting their broader canonical importance. The weighted final vote is a design adaptation, not the original Charter’s full legitimacy mechanism.

Author corrections retained

Factions decide together; no individual secret objectives are required. Every faction has independent choices that can create risk and reward without another faction’s consent. Legal restrictions and physical capability are separate. Cooperation should involve actual compromise, not an automatic best outcome for everyone.

A lost commercial contract is an acute opportunity cost. A surviving ship returns after useful service. Do not remove ownership leverage merely because an asset was committed. Readiness contributes both Janissariat and Ascendant leverage; unspent foresight contributes only to the latter. Three foresight tokens, at most two purchases, implies a minimum one-token remainder.

The first five-player plan used unanimous final ratification. Later author-approved experiments replaced that with weighted party signatures; the current draft uses the latter. Trial articles still require the three relevant consents. This distinction must survive all future exports.

Open questions and material outside this prototype

Still to establish through play: whether cooperation’s concessions are sufficiently even; whether a party chooses NONE; whether weighted ratification over army dissent produces a credible government; whether the six-minute trial phase fits human play; whether Military autonomy or joint authority is attractive when actually tested.

The larger conspiracy, Houses, Clarity transformation, succession forgery, five Pillars, strategic map, hidden transmissions and pre-human structures remain historical setting material outside this small scenario. They are preserved in the source archive, not introduced through surprise adjudication.

The separate GameSpec attempt describes a 24–30-player game and treats its source directory as canonical within that project. It is preserved under archive/legacy-gamespec as an older design line. This migration creates a separate Playtrace repository; it does not claim to validate or rewrite that GameSpec package.
