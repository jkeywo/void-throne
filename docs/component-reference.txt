The Void Throne — Component Reference

Use this document alongside the Player Rulebook and your faction brief. It describes your assets, the choices available and the state of the crisis. Exact operational calculations are kept by the facilitator. The Ascendant's powers are in their brief.

1. Assets and tracks

The three tracks run from 0 to 4. Their descriptions tell you what the current situation means. They do not guarantee the result of an order. The facilitator announces the new values and what happened after resolution.

Beacon

Beacon records the condition of the Narrow's maintained navigation and service infrastructure. It starts at 2. Maintenance and repair restore the route; use wears it down.

4 — Sound: The navigation network is in good order, with reliable signals and well-maintained service installations.

3 — Serviceable: The route supports regular passage, though traffic and neglected maintenance will wear it down.

2 — Fragile: The route is usable but vulnerable. Faults and exposed traffic make protection and upkeep pressing concerns.

1 — Deteriorating: Failing signals and service installations interrupt the route. Substantial work is needed to restore dependable passage.

0 — Dark: The navigation infrastructure has failed. The route needs restoration before normal service can resume.

Sorenne's Fall — Stability

Stability records whether the system's forty million people can obtain supplies and keep their civil services operating. It starts at 3. Relief and protection support the system; shortages, disrupted traffic and fighting put it under strain.

4 — Secure: Supplies are reliable and services can meet immediate needs.

3 — Functioning: The administration is keeping the system supplied.

2 — Strained: Shortages are affecting daily life.

1 — Failing: Severe shortages are disrupting essential services.

0 — Emergency: The system cannot meet its immediate needs without relief.

Stability is not loyalty to the Ascendant. People can be well supplied and still oppose the new government.

Readiness

Readiness records the Janissariat force's combat effectiveness and cohesion. It starts at 3. Fighting sustains its conditioning; inactivity allows it to fray. Guarding traffic and staying at home do not satisfy that need to fight. A weakened force may be destroyed rather than strengthened by combat.

4 — Battle-ready: Conditioning holds firm. The force is cohesive, disciplined and prepared for sustained fighting.

3 — Steady: Troops respond reliably to command and retain the cohesion won during the conquest.

2 — Fraying: Combat power remains, but conditioning and discipline are under strain.

1 — Unreliable: Units struggle to act together. Sending them into serious fighting risks breaking the force.

0 — Disordered: Conditioning has broken down. Commanders cannot rely on coordinated combat.

These descriptions explain the tracks; they add no further bonuses or penalties.

Wayfarer — Guild freight vessel

Assign Wayfarer to Council or Trade.

Council offers the vessel to Navigation for Relief, Military or Shared passage. The ship cannot also fulfil its commercial contract that month.

Trade sends Wayfarer on the Guild's commercial voyage without council permission. A successful voyage fulfils the month's contract. The route's condition and protection matter: an exposed ship may be lost, while an impassable route may keep it in port.

There is one commercial opportunity each round. Missing it is a lost contract, not the loss of the ship. A surviving Wayfarer returns for its next assignment; the missed opportunity does not return.

Lantern — Guild tender

Assign Lantern to Maintain or Council.

Maintain sends the navigation team to care for the route directly. Council offers the tender for a Repair operation or to accompany Shared passage; Navigation must still choose that order.

Lantern cannot maintain the route while accompanying a voyage or serving another council task. Protect its navigators: a lost team cannot be replaced during this game.

Spear — the 9th Pale Cohort

Assign Spear to Council, Raid or Stand down.

Council offers the cohort for a Campaign or Raid chosen by Security. Campaign needs military passage supplied by Navigation.

Raid launches an independent interception of a Sorenne Flag patrol, using Spear's own local craft. Both Janissariat players must agree, but no other party's permission is needed. The current Charter may make this a breach of duty.

Stand down keeps the cohort out of operations. It does not rebuild a lost force or sustain its conditioning through combat.

An independent Raid cannot also supply a council operation. A disabled Spear cannot act again.

Vigil — the Narrow garrison

Assign Vigil to Council or Home.

Council offers the garrison for Guard or an imperial Warrant. An effective Warrant uses Vigil ahead of the council's other demands.

Home keeps Vigil at the army's institutional bases and fulfils its duty under Military autonomy.

Vigil can perform only one task each round. Plan which authority will use it; it cannot supply both Guard and a Warrant.

Ownership and service

Council is an offer of service for the round, not a transfer of ownership. A surviving asset returns to its owner for the next assignment. Committing it gives up another use it could have had that month.

Lost assets remain lost during the game. Repair restores the route, not a disabled ship or military unit. Protection can reduce physical dangers; it cannot repair navigation infrastructure or remove the strain of using the route.

2. Navigation and Supply

Choose one order using assets offered to Council. An offer permits their use but does not decide the council's order. Voyages depend on the route being usable; an authorised plan can still fail if its assets or supporting arrangements are missing.

Relief

Use offered Wayfarer to carry supplies to Sorenne's Fall. Grey Petrel may provide the voyage if the Ascendant arranged Relief lift. This service carries civilian supplies, not Spear for a Campaign.

Military

Use offered Wayfarer to carry the army beyond the Narrow. Security must also commit offered Spear to Campaign for that attack to take place. This voyage gives military passage priority over civilian supply.

Shared

Use both offered Wayfarer and Lantern to carry civilian supplies and provide military passage on the same voyage. Combining the work limits the relief effort and places Lantern's navigation team with the convoy. Lantern cannot also maintain the route.

Repair

Use offered Lantern for concentrated work on the route. This operation carries no council cargo. Wayfarer may still pursue private Trade through the repaired route.

Hold

Make no council voyage or repair. Private Trade and Maintain assignments still go ahead where possible. Holding does not cancel an independent Raid.

Orders that cannot be carried out

You cannot command an asset its owner has not offered. Missing assets, an unusable route or incompatible commitments can defeat a plan and disrupt the system's supply arrangements. The facilitator resolves the consequences and reports what happened.

3. Security and Government

Choose one order using the assets offered to Council. An independent Raid or an imperial Warrant may also occur, but no asset can be used twice.

Campaign

Use offered Spear and delivered Military or Shared passage to attack the Sorenne Flag's anchorage beyond the Narrow. Relief cannot transport this Campaign. Fighting sustains the army's conditioning if the force can bear it, but an attack disrupts the supply traffic on which Sorenne's Fall depends. A weakened cohort risks destruction.

Raid

Use offered Spear to seek a Sorenne Flag patrol on this side of the crossing. An independent Raid uses Spear's own faction assignment instead. No Guild ship is required.

An interception may find no target. Even an unsuccessful expedition disrupts civilian traffic; finding an enemy gives a capable force combat, but puts a weakened force in danger. Foresight may reveal what brings the patrol within reach.

Guard

Use offered Vigil, if it is not already serving a Warrant, to protect traffic and service installations. This supports civilian life and protects voyages from some threats. Guard does not repair the route or give the army the fighting it needs.

Restraint

Order no council military operation. A separately assigned independent Raid still proceeds. Restraint is not recovery for a damaged force.

Conflicting demands

If Spear was assigned to an independent Raid, Security cannot also use it for Campaign. If Vigil serves a Warrant, it cannot also Guard for the council. Failed orders can disrupt the system even when another operation still takes place.

4. Provisional government

The first round ends with a chance to try a new government. Choose who can direct each council and what duties come with that authority. The articles that pass govern round two and cannot be revoked or replaced during it.

An article gives authority and imposes a legal service duty. It does not transfer ownership of an asset. A faction can disobey a duty and carry out an otherwise possible independent action, but the broken arrangements can harm Stability.

A Standing Commitment goes further: both owners bind a particular assignment for round two. Use it when another party wants a dependable offer of service before accepting an article.

Interim

Under the starting arrangement, the Ascendant chooses both council orders and may issue a Warrant. The Guild has no service duty. An independent Janissariat Raid breaks discipline and causes disruption beyond the expedition itself.

Navigation articles

Guild discretion (NG): the Guild Navigation delegate chooses the order. The Guild must assign Lantern to Maintain or provide a funded Repair. Neglecting this duty disrupts the system. Wayfarer may Trade.

Imperial priority (NI): the Ascendant chooses the order. The Guild must offer Wayfarer to Council; withholding it breaks the service duty. The ship forgoes Trade even if the council leaves it unused.

Joint authorisation (NJ): both Navigation delegates must agree the same order, or Navigation Holds. The Guild must offer Wayfarer to Council; withholding it breaks the service duty.

Offering Lantern without a funded Repair does not fulfil Guild discretion's upkeep duty. Grey Petrel providing Relief does not fulfil the Wayfarer duty under Imperial priority or Joint authorisation.

Military articles

Imperial command (MI): the Ascendant chooses Security’s order and retains Warrant power. The Janissariat must offer Spear to Council; withholding it breaks the service duty. The Ascendant may still choose not to fight.

Military autonomy (MA): the Janissariat Security delegate chooses the order. The Janissariat must keep Vigil Home; sending it elsewhere breaks the duty. There is no imperial Warrant. An independent Raid is permitted if Vigil stays Home.

Joint authorisation (MJ): both Security delegates must agree the same order, or Security uses Restraint. The Janissariat must offer Spear to Council; withholding it breaks the service duty. There is no imperial Warrant.

Lost assets do not waive a duty. Broken duties disrupt the civil administration’s supply and protection arrangements, alongside the consequences of operations and shortages.

Enacting an article

Navigation needs the Guild Navigation delegate, Janissariat Navigation delegate and Ascendant to agree the same exact proposal. Military needs the Guild Security delegate, Janissariat Security delegate and Ascendant. State agreement aloud to the facilitator; no signatures are needed.

The two articles are decided independently. One may pass while the other stays Interim. Agreeing an article does not by itself bind an asset promise made by your teammate.

Standing Commitments

Each faction may attach one available asset assignment to one exact article proposal. Both teammates must approve it. Permitted assignments are:

Wayfarer: Council.

Lantern: Council or Maintain.

Spear: Council.

Vigil: Council or Home.

Record the owner, asset, assignment and article, then ask both teammates to confirm the commitment aloud. If either owner does not agree, that article fails as proposed. To remove or change the promise, agree revised terms and obtain the necessary agreement again.

An article can pass without a commitment. A valid commitment activates only if its article passes. It then overrides contrary round-two selections and defaults, and expires after the round's operation.

The assignment is binding; the council's future order is not. An announced plan creates no extra mechanical penalty if the authorised council later chooses something else.

Example: the Guild attaches Lantern Council to a Joint military proposal. Both Guild players approve the promise; both Security delegates and the Ascendant approve the article. Lantern is now locked to Council next round, but Navigation must still decide whether to use it for Repair or Shared.

Missing or conflicting orders

An unconfirmed, ambiguous or unauthorised Navigation order becomes Hold. The equivalent Security order becomes Restraint. Under a joint article, conflicting choices count as disagreement.

A council without an authorised decision uses its default. An authorised order may still fail for lack of assets. Private assignments and Charter duties continue in either case.
