The Void Throne

Component Reference

Use this document alongside the Player Rulebook and your faction brief. It describes your assets, the choices available and the state of the crisis. Exact operational calculations are kept by the facilitator. The Ascendant's powers are in their brief.

1. Assets and tracks

The three tracks run from 0 to 4. Their descriptions tell you what the current situation means. They do not guarantee the result of an order. The facilitator announces the new values and what happened after resolution.

Beacon

Beacon records the condition of the Narrow's maintained navigation and service infrastructure. It starts at 2. Maintenance and repair restore the route; use wears it down.

  • 4 — Sound: The navigation network is in good order, with reliable signals and well-maintained service installations.
  • 3 — Serviceable: The route supports regular passage, though traffic and neglected maintenance will wear it down.
  • 2 — Fragile: The route is usable but vulnerable. Faults and exposed traffic make protection and upkeep pressing concerns.
  • 1 — Deteriorating: Failing signals and service installations interrupt the route. Substantial work is needed to restore dependable passage.
  • 0 — Dark: The navigation infrastructure has failed. The route needs restoration before normal service can resume.

Sorenne's Fall — Stability

Stability records whether the system's forty million people can obtain supplies and keep their civil services operating. It starts at 3. Relief and protection support the system; shortages, disrupted traffic and fighting put it under strain.

  • 4 — Secure: Supplies are reliable and services can meet immediate needs.
  • 3 — Functioning: The administration is keeping the system supplied.
  • 2 — Strained: Shortages are affecting daily life.
  • 1 — Failing: Severe shortages are disrupting essential services.
  • 0 — Emergency: The system cannot meet its immediate needs without relief.

Stability is not loyalty to the Ascendant. People can be well supplied and still oppose the new government.

Readiness

Readiness records the Janissariat force's combat effectiveness and cohesion. It starts at 3. Fighting sustains its conditioning; inactivity allows it to fray. Guarding traffic and staying at home do not satisfy that need to fight. A weakened force may be destroyed rather than strengthened by combat.

  • 4 — Battle-ready: Conditioning holds firm. The force is cohesive, disciplined and prepared for sustained fighting.
  • 3 — Steady: Troops respond reliably to command and retain the cohesion won during the conquest.
  • 2 — Fraying: Combat power remains, but conditioning and discipline are under strain.
  • 1 — Unreliable: Units struggle to act together. Sending them into serious fighting risks breaking the force.
  • 0 — Disordered: Conditioning has broken down. Commanders cannot rely on coordinated combat.

These descriptions explain the tracks; they add no further bonuses or penalties.

Wayfarer — Guild freight vessel

Traits: Civilian Transport; Troop Transport. Council: Navigation and Supply. Faction order: Fulfil Commercial Contract. Leave the card at home for that voyage, or take it to Navigation for council service.

Offer Council Service makes Wayfarer available to Navigation for Deliver Relief or Transport Troops. Choose one job for the ship. It cannot also complete a commercial contract that month.

Fulfil Commercial Contract sends Wayfarer on the Guild's own voyage. Successful delivery completes that month's contract. Poor navigation or an exposed route can halt the voyage or cost you the ship.

There is one commercial opportunity each round. A missed contract is an immediate lost opportunity, not a continuing penalty. A surviving Wayfarer returns to the Guild afterwards.

Lantern — Guild tender

Traits: Civilian Transport; Route Engineering. Council: Navigation and Supply. Faction order: Maintain the Narrows. Leave the card at home for routine upkeep, or take it to Navigation for council service.

Offer Council Service makes Lantern available to Navigation for Deliver Relief or Repair the Narrows. Lantern can carry civilian passengers and essential relief supplies, but cannot transport Spear. It cannot carry civilians and repair the route in the same round.

Maintain the Narrows sends the navigation team on routine upkeep without a council order. Council repair is a more concentrated operation. Neither assignment restores a lost asset.

Lantern's navigators are irreplaceable during the prototype. Taking them on a civilian voyage exposes them to the dangers of that passage.

Spear — the 9th Pale Cohort

Traits: Assault; Patrol Craft. Council: Security and Government. Two faction-order faces: Stand Down (default) / Intercept a Patrol. Leave the chosen face showing at home, or take the card to Security. Both faces show the same traits; neither faction order happens while the card is at a council.

Offer Council Service makes Spear available to Security for Attack the Anchorage or Intercept a Patrol. Attack the Anchorage also needs Navigation to deliver Transport Troops. Spear's local craft can intercept a patrol without Guild transport.

Intercept a Patrol as a faction assignment launches the same operation independently. Both Janissariat players agree that assignment; no other party's permission is needed. A current veto against Intercept a Patrol also bars this independent use. The Charter may impose a service duty which the independent assignment breaks.

Stand Down keeps Spear out of operations. It does not restore a lost cohort or sustain its conditioning through combat. An independent interception cannot also supply a council attack.

Vigil — the Narrow garrison

Trait: Passage Defence. Council: Security and Government. Faction order: Defend Army Bases. Leave the card at home to defend the bases, or take it to Security for council service.

Offer Council Service makes Vigil available to Security for Protect the Passage or an imperial Warrant. An effective Warrant uses it first. The garrison cannot perform both tasks in one round.

Defend Army Bases keeps Vigil at the army's institutional bases. It fulfils the duty under Military autonomy, but provides neither passage protection nor qualifying combat.

Grey Petrel — the Ascendant's contracted cutter

Traits: Civilian Transport; Troop Transport. Council: Navigation and Supply. Two faction-order faces: Retain the Cutter (default) / Protect the Navigators. Both faces show the same traits. Grey Petrel has a licensed Guild pilot and a protection detail, but not the Passage Defence trait: its specific rescue is not general route protection. The Ascendant controls one contracted service for the prototype, represented by the reserve token. Leave the chosen face showing at the Ascendant’s place, or give the card to Navigation before the faction bell. Council service replaces either faction order.

Offer Council Service makes Grey Petrel available to Navigation for either Deliver Relief or Transport Troops. GN records its allocation alongside the Guild ships after discussion with JN. It can carry civilians or the army, but can perform only one order. Using this service spends its token, even if poor conditions prevent departure. Offering it without using it leaves the token available.

Protect the Navigators holds Grey Petrel ready to rescue Lantern's navigation team from the threatened first-month civilian passage. The facilitator calls on it only if that specific threat occurs. If called upon, spend the token; otherwise keep it. This task provides no general passage protection, cannot also transport cargo or troops, and has no second-month trigger.

Retain the Cutter keeps the token without providing service. A missing assignment also means Retain the Cutter. A spent token makes Grey Petrel unavailable for the remainder of the prototype; unspent reserve adds no settlement weight. It cannot repair the Narrows, complete the Guild's contract or replace lost units.

Ownership and use

Faction Orders are the tasks you choose for assets kept at your faction table. Council Orders use assets brought to the council. The card faces show printed Faction Orders; use an Other marker for an agreed alternative. Keeping an asset at the faction table does not mean that it stays in port: a commercial voyage or independent interception is still a Faction Order.

The card’s location records its assignment. At its printed council it is available for orders matching its traits; at home it performs the action showing. No faction sheet is needed. Do not move a card between councils or send it home during councils. Each asset performs one task, and each printed order can be selected once per round. An unused council card does no faction order. A surviving faction asset returns home after resolution; Grey Petrel is limited by its one-service contract.

You can carry out every order for which the assets and requirements are available, unless that order is vetoed. There is no limit of one order per council. Navigation may arrange both transports using different ships, or repair while another ship transports. Security may attack or intercept with Spear while Vigil protects the passage. Spear cannot both attack and intercept.

Other — Faction Order

Requirement: name an objective and the available assets your faction will commit. Both teammates agree; the Ascendant decides for Grey Petrel. Keep the cards at your faction table and place an Other marker beside them. Each asset is used once, in place of its printed Faction Order.

Ask the facilitator to rule on the proposal before the faction deadline. They set requirements and consequences by comparison with existing orders. Other is never a cheaper, safer or stronger way to obtain the same result. It cannot supply another faction’s assets, create new traits, evade a veto or Standing Commitment, or replace cooperation that the operation would normally require. If the proposal needs a council operation, take the necessary cards to that council before the faction phase closes.

If no ruling is agreed before the deadline, choose a printed Faction Order; unresolved disagreement uses the normal faction defaults. An agreed Other order is resolved with the round’s other operations. It grants no extra use of an asset.

2. Navigation and Supply

Other — Council Order

Requirement: describe the intended action and outcome, and identify the offered assets it needs. The facilitator adjudicates it before the council deadline. Use each asset once; an asset used for Other cannot also perform another order.

Other cannot be more efficient than an existing order or avoid cross-faction coordination. All normal ownership, trait, transport, protection, veto and commitment requirements remain. An equivalent task counts as that printed order for single-use limits, prophecies and developments. Other does not create a second copy of a printed order. If no ruling is agreed in time, leave Other unselected; its proposed assets remain unused unless assigned to another legal order before the deadline.

Discuss the programme at Navigation. GN records which offered asset serves each selected order, including Grey Petrel if the Ascendant offered it. JN contributes the army's priorities and plans. No separate agreement from an authority holder is needed to carry out a funded, unvetoed order.

Deliver Relief

Requirement: one offered asset with Civilian Transport. Place its card beside this order.

Carry civilian passengers and essential supplies through the Narrow to support Sorenne's Fall. Lantern carries civilians, not the military cohort. The route must be usable for the voyage to depart.

Transport Troops

Requirement: one offered asset with Troop Transport. Place its card beside this order.

Move the army through the Narrow. Security must also allocate an Assault asset to Attack the Anchorage for that attack to take place. Transporting the troops does not itself count as combat or provide civilian relief.

Repair the Narrows

Requirement: one offered asset with Route Engineering, unused by another task.

Carry out concentrated repairs to navigation and service installations. Other ships can use the repaired route during the same round. This restores the route, not a damaged ship or cohort.

Selecting the programme

Select any combination you can supply with separate assets. Leave unwanted orders blank. Two transport orders require two vessels. There is no Shared order: choose Deliver Relief and Transport Troops separately. Either or both transports place strain on the route; the facilitator keeps the exact calculation concealed.

Orders lacking an offered asset, using an asset twice or struck out by a veto cannot be carried out. Clear conflicts before the deadline. If an asset's final allocation remains ambiguous, it does neither disputed job. Other valid orders and independent assignments still proceed. An operation can still fail after being selected because of the route's condition or another council's decision.

3. Security and Government

Other — Council Order

Requirement: describe the intended action and outcome, and identify the offered assets it needs. The facilitator adjudicates it before the council deadline. Use each asset once; an asset used for Other cannot also perform another order.

Other cannot be more efficient than an existing order or avoid cross-faction coordination. All normal ownership, trait, transport, protection, veto and commitment requirements remain. An equivalent task counts as that printed order for single-use limits, prophecies and developments. Other does not create a second copy of a printed order. If no ruling is agreed in time, leave Other unselected; its proposed assets remain unused unless assigned to another legal order before the deadline.

Discuss the programme at Security. JS records the offered army assets used for each order. GS contributes the Guild's concerns and the Ascendant can advise or issue a Warrant where permitted. None has an additional approval gate over funded, unvetoed orders.

Attack the Anchorage

Requirement: one offered asset with Assault, and delivered Transport Troops from Navigation.

Attack the Sorenne Flag's anchorage beyond the Narrow. Fighting sustains a capable force's conditioning, but disrupts the supplies on which Sorenne's Fall depends. A weakened cohort risks destruction. If passage does not arrive, the expedition cannot leave.

Intercept a Patrol

Requirement: one asset with Patrol Craft. Use an offered card at Security, or a card whose printed faction-order face permits this operation independently.

Use the force’s patrol craft to seek a Sorenne Flag patrol. The expedition may find no enemy, yet still disrupt civilian traffic. Finding an enemy provides combat for a capable force but risks destroying a weakened cohort. A standing veto bars both the council and independent versions of this order.

Protect the Passage

Requirement: one offered asset with Passage Defence, unused by an effective Warrant.

Protect traffic and service installations. Protection supports civilian life and reduces some dangers to voyages. It neither repairs the Narrows nor provides the army with combat. A veto against this order also bars using an imperial Warrant for the same protection.

Selecting the programme

Match an offered card’s traits to each selected order. An Assault asset can Attack the Anchorage if troop transport arrives; Patrol Craft can Intercept a Patrol; Passage Defence can Protect the Passage. Use each card once. Leaving orders blank does not cancel a legal faction order. Do not reuse an asset taken by an effective Warrant. Resolve unclear allocations before the deadline; unused council cards provide no automatic operation or faction order.

4. Authority and provisional government

During opening free discussion, authority lets its holder strike out an order for the council phase ahead. Record vetoes under the articles already in force, then agree any replacements. Show all vetoes before the faction phase begins. Authority does not let its holder choose another faction’s orders or requisition its assets.

Each entitled party may veto one printed order in each council where it holds a right, or choose None. Vetoes apply to the current round and expire after its operations. The Ascendant holds both opening vetoes in round one under Interim. A veto applies whatever asset would perform the operation. Changing an article does not erase a veto or grant an additional one in that opening phase.

Joint authority: contributor vetoes

Under a Joint article, every party that offered at least one available asset to either council during the previous round may issue its own veto in that Joint council. A Guild or Janissariat party gets one veto, not one per player or asset. The Ascendant qualifies by offering Grey Petrel to Navigation. The asset need only have been offered; it need not have been used or survived.

At Joint Navigation, GN exercises the Guild's veto and JN the Janissariat's. At Joint Military, GS exercises the Guild's and JS the Janissariat's. The Ascendant exercises their own. No other contributor's agreement is needed. Several parties may veto different orders; repeated vetoes against one order add nothing. If all orders are blocked, that council cannot order an operation this round.

Routine upkeep, a private contract, an independent interception, Defend Army Bases and Protect the Navigators do not count as offering an asset to a council. They give no contributor veto. An absent or spent asset cannot qualify a party. Contribution in the previous round to either council qualifies the party in each Joint domain.

Joint government therefore removes a single ruler's control but may produce several competing restrictions. Contributors must negotiate over which options to leave open. Its service duties still apply; authority does not remove a faction's obligations.

Interim

The Ascendant may veto one Navigation order and one Security order for the council phase ahead and may issue a Warrant. The Guild has no service duty. An independent Janissariat interception breaks imperial discipline, even when no veto prevents it.

Navigation articles

Guild discretion (NG): GN may veto one Navigation order for the council phase ahead. The Guild must assign Lantern to Maintain the Narrows or carry out Repair the Narrows. Wayfarer may pursue its contract.

Imperial priority (NI): the Ascendant may veto one Navigation order for the council phase ahead. The Guild must assign Wayfarer to Offer Council Service, forgoing its contract even if Navigation leaves it unused.

Joint authorisation (NJ): each contributing party may veto one Navigation order for the council phase ahead. The Guild must assign Wayfarer to Offer Council Service. Vetoes need not agree and can block different orders.

Military articles

Imperial command (MI): the Ascendant may veto one Security order for the council phase ahead and retains Warrant power. The Janissariat must assign Spear to Offer Council Service. A single veto cannot bar both kinds of fighting.

Military autonomy (MA): JS may veto one Security order for the council phase ahead. The Janissariat must assign Vigil to Defend Army Bases. There is no imperial Warrant. Spear may intercept independently unless a current veto bars it.

Joint authorisation (MJ): each contributing party may veto one Security order for the council phase ahead. The Janissariat must assign Spear to Offer Council Service. There is no imperial Warrant. Several vetoes may block different operations.

Service duties

An article imposes a duty, not a transfer of ownership. A faction can withhold a required asset and take a permitted independent assignment, but breaking the duty disrupts the system. Lost assets do not waive duties. A veto against an order does not waive an assignment duty or an existing Standing Commitment.

Under Guild discretion, merely offering Lantern does not fulfil upkeep: maintain or carry out repair. Under Imperial priority or Joint Navigation, Grey Petrel's service does not substitute for offering Wayfarer.

Enacting an article

Each opening Free Phase lets you enact or replace articles for that round. Navigation requires GN, JN and the Ascendant to agree the exact proposal aloud. Military requires GS, JS and the Ascendant. The facilitator records the agreement; no signatures are needed. Decide each article separately. A failed amendment leaves the existing article in force. If no proposal is made before the deadline, keep the existing articles.

Record vetoes first, using the existing articles and the previous round’s contributions. In round one there are no previous contributions, and both articles start Interim. Newly enacted articles govern that round’s duties and Warrant power, but do not grant another opening veto. An article enacted in round one can therefore govern vetoes at the start of round two. This tests its authority as well as its service duties.

Standing Commitments

Each faction may attach one available asset assignment to one article proposal. Both teammates must approve the exact owner, asset, assignment and article together. Allowed assignments are:

  • Wayfarer: Offer Council Service.
  • Lantern: Offer Council Service or Maintain the Narrows.
  • Spear: Offer Council Service.
  • Vigil: Offer Council Service or Defend Army Bases.

An unapproved attached commitment prevents its article passing as proposed. If the article and commitment pass, the assignment is locked for the current round and overrides contrary choices or defaults. It expires after that round's operations. An article can pass without a commitment; informal plans do not bind later orders.

Mark a promised placement or faction order on the card with a commitment marker. The promise fixes that assignment, not its eventual order. An asset may be left unused if no legal operation remains. Grey Petrel's assignment is made by the Ascendant each faction phase; it is not an additional faction Standing Commitment.
