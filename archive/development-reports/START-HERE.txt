THE VOID THRONE - PAPER PROTOTYPE 0.2
13 October 2026

USE THE FILES MARKED v0.2. The unversioned player/facilitator files and the
v0.1 ZIP are the previous prototype. The old simulation and lore review
remain historical records of that earlier version.

Five players + one facilitator; 60 minutes; two rounds and a settlement.
Each round represents one month. Gameplay uses paper only.

PRINT AND SET UP
1. Facilitator reads the seven-page facilitator pack, including spoilers.
2. Print the 15-page player pack and seven-page facilitator pack single-sided
   A4 at 100%: 22 pages total.
3. Cut player pages 7-12 and facilitator page 5 as marked.
4. Give two players the Guild brief and its two asset cards; give two players
   the Janissariat brief and its two asset cards. One member of each faction
   sits at each council. The Ascendant sits at Security.
5. Give the Ascendant their brief and worksheet. Use a coin for Grey Petrel.
6. Put three counters on Beacon 2, Communities 3, Readiness 3. All four faction
   assets begin ready. Commercial contracts begin at 0.
7. Record preferred articles before play. Bring pens and an ordinary clock.

WHAT CHANGED
Concrete ships and formations replace abstract Navigator/Force capacity.
Conditioned military power declines without successful combat. The crown's
independent Warrant depends on Readiness and an offered garrison. Factions
can act independently; laws impose costs instead of cancelling attempts.
Articles impose service duties, foregone work and shared authority.

CHECKS AND LIMITS
26 named mechanical assertions and 46,080 combinations of single-round
choices checked. Both PDFs rendered and inspected (22 pages). These are desk
checks, not a new six-agent simulation or a human playtest. Balance, enjoyment
and resolving within the six-minute discussion phase remain to be tested.

FIRST PLAYTEST QUESTIONS
Did every party give something up? Was conflict a credible alternative?
Did the named assets clarify costs? Could both delegates contribute?
Did a vision change a decision? Did practical experience change a preferred
Charter article? Time the facilitator's resolution before adding more rules.
