The Void Throne

PLAYER GUIDE | VERSION 0.2 | 13 OCTOBER 2026

We have won an empire. Now we must decide what we are prepared to keep doing to hold it.

By John Keyworth, developed with Codex. Five players, one facilitator, 60 minutes. A paper game: use cards, pens and three counters. No electronic equipment is needed to play.

The situation

You meet at the Seam. Reports from the Narrow, a route serving the Vethis region, describe a failing beacon installation, a stranded field cohort and communities living on diminishing stores. Armed Conclave remnants threaten traffic. The occupation of Vethis has already lasted fourteen years. Its civilian opposition is not the remnant force.

Each round represents one month of operations before the final Ceremony. The eight-minute briefing opens this period of provisional government; it is not a literal day of instant interstellar travel. The local emergency and all named assets in this pack are new prototype scenario material.

The settlement

You negotiate the two outstanding Navigation and Military instruments of the wider Charter. For this scenario, the other institutions have accepted all six printed alternatives. The Ecclesiatum will sanctify any package ratified by the three played parties, and the remaining formalities are secured. No additional veto will appear.

From commitment to consequence

PLAYER GUIDE | PHASES AND AUTHORITY

1. Faction discussion - five minutes

Agree both asset assignments on your faction card. Both teammates sign. Put each asset beside its selected assignment. The bell locks these choices. A council can use only assets offered to it; an imperial directive cannot seize a withheld asset. Discuss your priorities before your delegates separate.

The Ascendant privately chooses this round's Navigation or Security vision and may lodge the Grey Petrel on one printed intervention. Both choices lock at the faction bell. A missed vision means none; a missing intervention means Keep. An untriggered reserve returns at round end.

2. Councils - seven minutes

Navigation chooses one order. Security chooses one order. Circle the order and obtain the initials required by the authority at the start of the round. The Ascendant may send and revise written directives until the bell. The final council card is binding. Spoken instructions and superseded notes have no effect.

Round one: the Ascendant chooses both operational orders. Each council also proposes one provisional article. Both delegates and the Ascendant must separately sign it. It takes effect in round two. Without all three, that domain remains Interim.

Round two: N-G gives Navigation to the Guild; N-I to the Ascendant; N-J to both delegates. M-I gives Security to the Ascendant; M-A to the Janissariat; M-J to both delegates. Interim means Ascendant. No mid-round article change is allowed.

Under joint authority, both delegates must initial. Missing, multiple or unauthorised orders default to Hold at Navigation or Restraint at Security. There is no penalty for invalidity alone. An otherwise valid order missing its required asset defaults and causes Communities -1. Committed assets cannot be reassigned after a default.

3. Free discussion - six minutes

The facilitator resolves both councils and independent assignments together, announces effects and returns the cards. After round one, post the provisional articles. Use the remaining time to negotiate. Promises do not move assets or change completed orders.

Law and physical power

The Guild can send Wayfarer on its own commercial voyage. The Janissariat can order an independent Raid. Neither requires another player's approval. A Charter obligation can make the act a breach; it does not prevent the attempt. Each faction that breaks its printed duty causes Communities -1, at most once per round. This stacks with operational effects.

Each asset has one assignment per round. Vigil cannot serve a Warrant and a council Guard. A Grey Petrel Relief lift can operate alongside Wayfarer's private voyage: these are two ships and cause two points of beacon wear.

Defaults

An unsigned or missing Guild card means Wayfarer Trade; Lantern Maintain. An unsigned or missing Janissariat card means Spear Stand down; Vigil Home. A missing or multiple asset selection uses that asset's default. Absent applies only to lost/disabled assets; it is not a voluntary choice. Their duties can still be broken. No faction is eliminated.

Communities3Local stores and confidence in the settlement.

Prepare the route, then use it

First add Lantern Maintain +1 Beacon, or a funded Repair +2 Beacon. These cannot both use Lantern. Limit this prepared value to 0-4. A voyage needs prepared Beacon 2+. Below 2 a council voyage halts, gives no cargo or military passage and causes Communities -1. A private Trade simply returns without a contract. Neither causes wear.

Each council voyage that crosses causes Beacon -1. A private Trade that crosses causes a further -1, even if attacked. Check all voyages against the same prepared value. Apply wear after every voyage is checked. There are no fees, abstract capacity points or transfers between assets.

Local survival and military power

Every round: Communities -1 as stores run down. Then add all cargo, Guard, operation, breach and hidden effects. Limit Communities to 0-4 once, at the end.

If Spear actually fights through a successful Campaign or Raid, Readiness +1. Otherwise Readiness -1. Apply this once per round and limit to 0-4. Use opening Readiness for strength checks and Warrants. Routine Guard is protection and deterrence, not a conditioning exercise.

A Campaign needs delivered Military or Shared passage. It causes Communities -1 and fights successfully at opening Readiness 2+. Below 2, Spear is overmatched and disabled. Without delivered passage, the Campaign does not leave: Communities -1; no combat. Spear remains intact.

A Raid costs Communities -1 for disruption even if it misses. Whether the remnant target appears is a hidden fixed condition. If present and opening Readiness is 2+, it fights and causes a further Communities -1. If present below 2, Spear is disabled; no further Community loss. A missing target does not disable Spear.

Independent trade and physical protection

Wayfarer Trade completes one commercial contract if prepared Beacon is 3+, or if it is 2 and Vigil provides Guard/Warrant protection. At 2 without protection, attackers disable Wayfarer and the contract fails. Its crew survives. A contract is recorded on the board; it cannot be spent, gifted or converted into another asset.

A Guard or valid Warrant uses Vigil, gives Communities +1 and supplies guarded status for the round. It protects traffic and crews against specified physical threats. It does not raise Beacon or enable passage below 2. Hidden effects may also depend on the final funded orders.

Damage: a disabled asset or lost crew is unavailable for the rest of this prototype, including the ending. Surviving assets return next round. No replacement, retraining or recovery action exists in this short scenario.

Ductus Guild

SHARED BRIEF | TWO PLAYERS

We can carry your army, your food or our own obligations. The same ship cannot be in three places.

You operate the local passage service. Your navigators learn over decades to interpret the immaterial deep; soldiers cannot substitute for them. The immediate problem is the Narrow's worn physical beacon and the demands placed on its ships, not a spell that repairs the void.

Starting assetsUse
Wayfarer - freight vesselOffer to Navigation, or complete a private commercial contract.
Lantern - survey tenderOffer to Navigation, or Maintain the beacon independently.

Your shared priorities

Protect your people: keep Lantern's navigation team alive and finish with Beacon 2+.
Protect your institution: secure a formal answer to GUILD-14 and protected Guild navigation rights. Prefer N-G's discretion; N-J trades that discretion for a voice over every Navigation order. N-I recognises the licence but leaves traffic priorities to the crown.
Meet your obligations: two commercial contracts are due, one each round. Record how many you complete. Neither paid work nor safe passage should be taken for granted.
Keep the region viable: aim for Communities 2+.

A petition, not a charity charter

Your navigation rights petition has waited fourteen years. The monopoly concerns expertise, licensing and associated services. The articles in this prototype settle licensed passage and duty of service; wider communications rights remain reserved to a later instrument. Every printed option gives a formal response, but on different terms.

You do not automatically give civilian cargo priority. You may do so because people need help, the region sustains your business, or someone offers an acceptable bargain. A compulsory military service duty is a real concession even when you willingly carry troops today.

At the two tables

Your Navigation delegate bargains over the ships and the navigation instrument. Your Security delegate can secure Guard for a risky private voyage or for Lantern's exposed crew, and can negotiate limits on the army's activity. Write one request for each delegate before separating.

Choices you can make alone

Wayfarer Trade uses your own ship and needs no outsider's permission. Lantern Maintain likewise acts without council approval. Both may leave a council order without its required asset. Trade can succeed while imperial relief fails; it can also expose your ship to attack if protection and beacon preparation are inadequate.

You cannot retrain a lost navigation team during this game. Returning perception in veterans is a possible subject for future research, not a supply of qualified navigators.

Janissariat

SHARED BRIEF | TWO PLAYERS

They ask what our soldiers will do when the fighting ends. We are beginning to ask the same question.

Your conditioning was made for war. Without qualifying fighting it frays, weakening combat power and loosening the habits that made the army a dependable imperial instrument. Soldiers begin questioning and sensing things they were trained to suppress. That may be a future for your people. It is a threat to the institution you currently command.

Starting assetsUse
Spear - the 9th Pale CohortOffer to Security, launch an independent Raid, or Stand down. It has local assault craft; an external Campaign needs Guild passage.
Vigil - the Narrow garrisonOffer to Security and possible imperial Warrant, or remain Home. Home is an institutional duty, not a Readiness bonus.

Your shared priorities

Keep a capable force: aim for Readiness 3+, preferably 4, and an intact Spear cohort.
Secure your institution: prefer M-A's autonomous order. M-J buys legitimacy but exposes campaigns to another institution's veto. M-I accepts that the crown can direct you to stand down.
Protect your people: do not casually spend Spear at opening Readiness below 2. Conditioning is not the same thing as the welfare of the soldiers who carry it.
Leave somewhere worth defending: aim for Communities 2+.

Your military choices

A Campaign seeks a named armed remnant position beyond the route. It requires Military or Shared passage. A Raid uses Spear's own local craft to intercept an armed remnant detachment on this side of the crossing. It needs no Guild transport, but the target may not appear. Neither action is an attack on the civilian opposition of Vethis.

Actual successful contact sustains conditioning in this prototype. This deliberately narrows the larger game's deployment/internal-trials rule for the author's requested fighting incentive. No approved internal-trials site is available in this local emergency; its costs and legitimacy are outside this test. Guard, food deliveries and orders that never reach a fight do not qualify.

Authority and loyalty

The Ascendant needs your army and its loyalty while it fights. Low Readiness weakens their ability to use Vigil through an imperial Warrant. You are not instructed to betray the throne if conditioning frays. Your players decide what loyalty means in the resulting settlement.

An independent Raid needs only your faction's signatures. It breaches Interim, M-I or M-J duties; under M-A it is permitted, provided Vigil stays Home. A council Raid is a separate lawful order using offered Spear. Do not assign Spear to both.

Your Navigation delegate secures passage or influences the traffic that might draw a target out. Your Security delegate decides military priorities and constitutional terms. Agree what each is authorised to promise.

The Ascendant

SOLE PLAYER BRIEF

A peace upheld by this army must answer what the army will fight tomorrow.

You want a future that outlives conquest. Your authority rests partly on an army whose conditioning needs fighting. Continued violence may preserve your military foundation while damaging the future you are trying to secure.

Your priorities

Preserve a workable empire: ratify the two instruments with Beacon 1+, Communities 2+ and Readiness 2+.
Keep the army you need: prefer Readiness 3+ and an intact Spear. At low Readiness your independent Warrant capability is lost.
Retain room to govern: prefer at least one imperial article. Sharing both domains is a substantial constitutional concession.
Account for your rule: name the people who paid for the settlement you secured.

Foresight

In each faction phase choose Navigation or Security. Read that round's matching vision; the other remains unseen. The card reveals fixed consequences conditional on choices, never what another player will choose. You may show, describe, conceal or misrepresent it. The facilitator does not certify your claims to the others.

Every printed consequence applies whether its vision is read or not. No rewind, extra question or improvised prophecy changes the rules. Averting a condition means the prophecy was successfully avoided, not that it was false.

Imperial Warrant - an army-backed power

Requirement: Interim or M-I military authority, opening Readiness 3+, and Vigil offered to Security.
Cost: assign Vigil to the Warrant before the council bell.
Effect: Communities +1 and guarded status. Security may still use offered Spear for a different order. It cannot also use Vigil for Guard.

If you issue a Warrant under Interim/M-I but lack Readiness or offered Vigil, it fails: Communities -1. Outside those articles it is only advice, with no effect or penalty. Under M-J the delegates may choose Guard; you have lost the power to order it independently. Your formal title cannot replace military support.

One prepared intervention

Your reserve is the Grey Petrel, a contracted light cutter with its own licensed Guild pilot and a small protection detail. Its contract covers one intervention during the entire game. Before councils, lodge Relief lift, Tender cover or Keep on your worksheet. The exact triggers are printed there. The cutter cannot carry the cohort, repair the beacon or solve both councils' problems.

Your place in government

You attend Security and send public written directions to Navigation where you hold authority. Every provisional article needs your assent; the final package needs your signature. You cannot revoke provisional powers during round two. Delegation changes what you can do, not merely what title appears on a card.

At the deadline

All approvals present: the instruments are ratified. Their continuing rights and duties enter the wider Charter; prearranged sanctification and formalities follow. No extra round or recovery occurs.
Any approval missing: the provisional articles expire. Interim legal arrangements return. Existing damage, losses, contracts and tracks remain. Low Readiness still weakens imperial enforcement; a title does not restore the army.

Debrief - six minutes

2. What did each party give up for cooperation? Was any concession merely cosmetic?
3. Was unilateral action a credible alternative? What made it attractive or dangerous?
4. Did a named asset make a promise or loss feel concrete? Which card was confusing?
5. Did the Ascendant learn something useful without solving both tables alone?
