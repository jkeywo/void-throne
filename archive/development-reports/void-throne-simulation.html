The outcome

A successful peace, with a less powerful throne

The players supplied the settlements and stranded army, preserved every navigator and improved the void route. They settled on joint Navigation and joint Military authority (N-J / M-J). The Ascendant secured a workable peace by surrendering both operational powers.

What this trial suggests

The faction–council interaction worked: a cheaper escort let the Janissariat rest troops, while the Ascendant's bridge let the Guild maintain the route and still transport supplies. The political bargain changed who made the decisions. Foresight was less consistent: the first vision gave a concrete danger, but the second had no applicable condition at the current Route value.

The final settlement attracted little resistance once the joint system succeeded. This is evidence from one cooperative agent simulation, not proof of balance or of how a human group will behave.

How to read this report

These are agent-generated player statements preserved from the contribution files, not quotations from a human playtest. Each role made its own choices. Tabs separate simultaneous faction meetings and councils; the report reader can inspect private Communion and faction conversations. Gold boxes record proposals, signatures, locked commitments and resolved effects. A signature alone does not mean an order has resolved.

The minute labels follow the intended 60-minute session schedule. They are a logical game clock, not measured speaking time. The transcript includes written relays between tables; it does not test whether humans could manage that volume of communication within seven minutes.

The game itself remains entirely on paper. The HTML is a simulation report, not a required playing aid.

What to test with people next

Keep this as a baseline before changing several rules at once. The most useful next test is whether real participants find and trust the same cooperation under the clock.

  • Make every selected vision offer a relevant choice. The second Navigation card has a gap at Route 2. Test a revised card with a consequence that can matter there, while retaining reliable conditions and one-card selection.
  • Watch for early agreement and surplus supplies. The final convoy produced two civilian points and one army point beyond the caps. M-J's discount and aligned goals made the bargain attractive. Check whether this feels satisfying or makes the Ceremony a formality before changing costs.
  • Test a disagreement under joint authority. Both joint councils agreed quickly. The live run did not test vetoes, default orders, withheld resources, conflicting directives or a refused Charter.
  • Measure communication at the tables. The simulation used several written relays and explicit confirmation of signatures. Observe whether both delegates remain involved and whether the bell interrupts worthwhile bargaining.
  • Ask about foresight separately from reserve power. The bridge clearly enabled the final plan; the evidence for foresight changing decisions is weaker. Do not treat those as the same success.

Method and limits

Six logical roles were run through separate agent histories: Guild Navigation, Guild Security, Janissariat Navigation, Janissariat Security, Ascendant and facilitator. This session continued to reject a fourth active subagent after the concurrency setting was changed, so the roles were scheduled in batches of up to three. They were not six simultaneously running agents. The original Ascendant and Guild Navigation roles were resumed in replacement agent histories after interruption, using their saved contributions.

The coordinating assistant routed messages, preserved the contribution files, checked the arithmetic and assembled this report. It did not play a seventh character. Players were instructed to read only public rules, their own faction brief and explicitly routed messages; the facilitator had the complete consequence table. This was instruction-based information separation in a shared workspace, not enforced filesystem isolation. Statements of reasons are the agents' concise explanations, not hidden reasoning traces.
