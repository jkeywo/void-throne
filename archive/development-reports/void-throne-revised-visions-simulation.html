Rules fixed before play

The Ascendant starts with three foresight tokens. Each vision costs one; declining costs nothing. At most one vision per round. Final signature weights are Guild: 2 for intact Wayfarer + 1 for living Lantern crew + 1 for Beacon 1+; Janissariat: Readiness; Ascendant: Readiness + unspent foresight. A package needs two-thirds of all available leverage rounded up, with at least two positive-weight parties signing. A two-player faction signs only when both agree. There is no inherent final veto.

Provisional articles retain their existing assent requirements and optional named asset commitments. Surviving assets return after their service; a missed commercial voyage is an acute opportunity cost. The Ascendant seeks a dependable government that can outlast them, with no objective to retain an imperial article.

All five personalities and objectives match the previous weighted-settlement run; five fresh player agents make independent choices. At minute 2, factions and Ascendant display draft intentions and both current-round vision covers. At minute 3 the Ascendant buys one named answer or declines. Final faction assignments and reserve lock at minute 5, after a chance to react. Existing Standing Commitments stay binding. Draft orders do not bind councils. This is one fresh run, not a controlled causal experiment. Private tabs are visible to the report reader; player access was scoped. Printed times are the proposed paper clock, not measured human play time.

Round one: a live question produced a different plan

Before paying, the Ascendant proposed Military passage, Campaign and a Warrant. Both Guild players displayed Trade/Maintain with Hold/Guard requests; the army offered its assets but asked for Military/Campaign. After seeing those drafts, the Ascendant selected the Security cover to investigate a Raid as an alternative to the unavailable passage. The answer revealed that completed private Trade brings the target. The Ascendant then proposed Relief using Grey Petrel, Raid and Warrant. All four faction players changed their council requests and explicitly cited the disclosed condition. The final authorised operations followed that revised plan. No asset assignment changed.

The first purchase had an observable consequence

Grey Petrel supplied Relief while Wayfarer completed its private contract. The Raid found its target and sustained conditioning. Communities ended at 3 and Readiness at 4, with all faction assets intact. Two crossings left Beacon at 1 and spent the reserve. The information did not create these options or add a bonus: it identified a reliable condition on which the players could base their bargain. Round two: relevant information, limited change of action

The new state made Repair important, and the Guild had bound Lantern to Council under provisional NG. The displayed plan combined Repair and private Trade, exactly the combination named on the Navigation cover. The Ascendant bought that answer, learning its extra Communities +1. They retained their pre-purchase Repair advice, Guard order and no-Warrant position; both Guild players retained their assignments and requests, explicitly describing greater confidence. The army changed its request from Restraint with a Warrant to Guard without one, citing equivalent protection and easier coordination. The extra supply benefit would apply even if unread. The evidence supports confirmation, better accounting and coordination more strongly than a second major operational change.

No deliberate saving occurred

Both decisions were explicit purchases: Security in round one, Navigation in round two. Tokens went 3→2→1. The Ascendant recognised the lost signature weight and preferred information relevant to the displayed plans. In their debrief, they judged the second purchase less useful in practice and said declining would have been defensible; that is hindsight, not a choice they made. At most two tokens can be spent in this prototype, so the last token is guaranteed. This run demonstrates more informed selection of cards; it does not demonstrate choosing NONE or establish how often real players will save.

The forgone leverage mattered

At the actual ending the weights were Guild 4, Janissariat 3 and Ascendant 4, with 8 required. Only Guild–Ascendant could pass as a two-party coalition. With the same physical ending and one purchase declined, Ascendant weight would be 5 and the threshold would still be 8; Ascendant–Janissariat would also qualify. That is arithmetic, not a simulated alternative ballot or evidence that the army and Ascendant would have agreed.

Constitution and compromise

Guild navigation and joint military authorisation passed with 8/11 leverage (threshold 8). Signing parties: Guild, Ascendant; refusing parties: Janissariat. Guild discretion was the only provisional article enacted: its Lantern Council commitment supplied the subsequent Repair. Military autonomy failed on the Guild security delegate’s refusal even though the Ascendant approved testing it. The army accepted a noncombat second round, reducing both its own and the Ascendant’s Readiness-based weight by one. The Guild completed both commercial contracts this time; its concession was the binding Lantern commitment and delivery of public upkeep, not a lost Wayfarer contract. The Ascendant paid for both visions and the reserve service. Costs were real but unevenly distributed.

Comparison with the previous run

The same five personalities and objectives were used with five fresh player agents and a fresh facilitator. Previously, the Ascendant bought Navigation twice and neither selected special condition applied to the chosen orders. This time, both purchased conditions were relevant and occurred: the first Raid target and the Repair/Trade distribution benefit. The physical ending was Beacon 2, Communities 4, Readiness 3, two contracts and a spent reserve, compared with 2/3/3, one contract and an unused reserve previously. This comparison does not isolate the effect of public covers from draft visibility, earlier disclosure, fresh choices or the different path of play.

Limits and what remains to test

All six roles authored their own contributions. Independent checks cover original drafts, exact card delivery after payment, later faction signatures, operational consequences and constitutional votes. Conversations were bounded and scheduled in batches; the stated 60 minutes is the proposed paper schedule, not measured human playing time. No assignment was revised after either answer, so the new opportunity to change asset commitments was not exercised. NG was tested operationally; no alternative military article was enacted provisionally. The second vision’s extra Community is a round-two scenario effect, not a permanent constitutional entitlement. This single run leaves deliberate saving and human timing unresolved.
