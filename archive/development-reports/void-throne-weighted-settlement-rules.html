The Void Throne — weighted settlement experiment
Experimental paper rules. Five players, one facilitator; two operational rounds and a Ceremony. These rules supersede conflicting v0.2 and Trial A wording. Operational costs and hidden consequences remain unchanged.

1. Foresight is personal power
Start: give the Ascendant THREE foresight tokens. These are separate from Grey Petrel. No tokens return during this game.
During each faction phase choose Navigation, Security or None. Choosing a vision costs ONE token, removed immediately before the facilitator shows that round's selected card. At most one vision may be read per round. With no tokens, choose None. A missing or invalid choice means None and costs nothing. You may not inspect a card before deciding, refund it, swap it, or buy the other card. Unspent tokens contribute to settlement leverage. Token expenditure and the number remaining are public; the chosen domain and card contents are private until the Ascendant elects to disclose them.
The reserve intervention still locks separately at the faction bell. If you decline a vision, you still choose Keep, Relief lift or Tender cover normally. All printed hidden consequences still apply whether anyone sees a vision or not.

2. Count the power behind each signature
Guild leverage: 2 for an intact Wayfarer, 1 for a living Lantern navigation team, and 1 for the Narrow at Beacon 1 or above. Maximum 4. No points for contracts, money, promises or an asset merely being assigned to public service.
Janissariat leverage: current Readiness, from 0 to 4.
Ascendant leverage: current Readiness PLUS unspent foresight tokens. No additional inherent veto at the Ceremony.
Readiness is counted in both political weights. This does not duplicate military units or pay for two operations with one asset. A one-point Readiness loss reduces both parties' leverage by one.
Show a public leverage record at briefing, after each foresight expenditure, after each operational resolution, and at the Ceremony. Freeze final weights from the actual post-round-two state and remaining foresight. No further foresight, operations or transfers occur at the Ceremony. Leverage cannot be donated or borrowed.

3. Ratify one complete settlement
One signature per PARTY: Guild, Janissariat, Ascendant. Both Guild players must approve the same exact package for the Guild to sign; both Janissariat players must do likewise. A divided, missing or explicitly refusing faction contributes zero support. Its full available weight remains in the denominator. The Ascendant decides its own signature. Record all five player decisions to establish these three signatures.
Pass threshold: at least TWO-THIRDS of ALL available leverage, rounded UP to a whole point, AND signatures from at least TWO parties with positive leverage. No party can ratify alone. A zero-total electorate cannot ratify. A party with zero leverage may record political approval but does not satisfy the two-party requirement.
Example starting weights: Guild 4, Janissariat 3, Ascendant 6, total 13, threshold 9. These numbers change with actual play. This is a calibration for this experiment, not established balance.
Each ballot contains one printed Navigation alternative and one printed Military alternative. There is no partial final ratification. Use fresh explicit decisions if either article changes. Signatures cannot carry to a revised package. No absent party automatically signs. A valid weighted majority may ratify despite one party's refusal. This enacts the printed powers and duties; it does not transfer ownership, compel automatic asset surrender, create resources or erase the normal breach consequences.
If the package passes, its printed articles become final, including the duties of a dissenting faction. If no package passes by the deadline, any enacted provisional articles expire and both domains revert to Interim. Physical outcomes and unspent foresight are unchanged. The Ascendant's remaining actual power is not replenished by a restored title.

4. Provisional government and temporary service
Keep Trial A: agree provisional articles after round-one resolution; each requires both relevant council representatives and the Ascendant. Each faction may voluntarily attach one available asset assignment, signed by BOTH owners, to one exact article. Unsigned attached commitments prevent that article passing. Approval and commitments are independent by domain.
The public round-two operational plan is a political promise. Activated Standing Commitments bind an assignment, not an operational order. They expire after round-two resolution. Every surviving asset returns to its owner. A Wayfarer Council commitment foregoes one trading opportunity and can deliver useful service; it neither destroys the ship nor permanently reduces Guild leverage. Lost or disabled assets remain lost or disabled under the existing consequences.
All unpledged cards retain their normal independent choices. Provisional rules do not use weighted signatures in this experiment.

5. The Ascendant's purpose
Seek a workable empire: a ratified settlement with Beacon 1+, Communities 2+ and Readiness 2+. Prefer Readiness 3+ and an intact Spear, but weigh military strength against civilian harm and the government being built.
Your legacy is a government that can sustain essential routes, protect communities and manage the army without repeated personal foresight and intervention. There is no requirement or reward for retaining an imperial article or both council powers. You may accept delegation when the actual evidence gives you confidence; you need not trust an untested promise or agree merely to produce a successful ending. Explain what gives or withholds that confidence.
Personal leverage lets you shape a dependable settlement. Spending foresight may help build that government while reducing your later bargaining power. Preserving foresight may strengthen your signature while leaving a consequence unknown.
