THE VOID THRONE · PROVISIONAL GOVERNMENT EXPERIMENT

A government worth trying

The first package produced a working provisional article under unanimous assent: Guild navigation authority in exchange for a locked month of Wayfarer service. The reduced-assent alternative was not needed for this test.

The test decided in advance

Try the unanimous-assent package first. It succeeds if at least one provisional article takes effect and its power or duty is exercised during round two. If none is exercised, test the alternative requiring the Ascendant and one council delegate. Permanent ratification still requires all five players.

Each test uses five player agents and one facilitator, with the hard-bargaining personalities from the earlier second run. Private knowledge is scoped to each role. Operational rules, assets and hidden consequences remain v0.2. Article timing and voluntary commitments are experimental changes. Communication now explicitly gives concrete offers and replies a place before the final signatures. This is a test of the combined package, not proof of which individual change caused an outcome.

Times are the intended 60-minute paper schedule. The agents did not play against a real clock. Tabs separate parallel conversations; green boxes separate mechanical submissions and phase-end records. Authored messages and delivery limitations are preserved.

What made the offer credible

The first proposal attached Lantern upkeep to Guild navigation. Daro, the Janissariat Navigation delegate, objected that this left the relief vessel free to trade. The replacement attached Wayfarer Council to the Navigation article. Both Guild owners accepted the lost trading opportunity, and GN, JN and the Ascendant all approved the article. This is the clearest evidence that a named commitment made a constitutional concession easier to accept. The run tests the combined package; it does not isolate the effect of timing from commitments.

The trial changed actual play

Round two began with Guild navigation authority and an irrevocably offered Wayfarer. GN alone signed Relief; Lantern Maintain met the new upkeep duty. The Ascendant treated its navigation request as advice and chose Guard under the remaining Interim military authority. Communities recovered from 1 to 3. No resources were created and no new cooperation bonus was paid.

The costs were uneven

The Guild finished with one contract, having surrendered its second-round trading opportunity. The Ascendant surrendered navigation authority for the trial. The Janissariat accepted a quiet month, with Readiness falling from 4 to 3, while freely withholding Spear and offering Vigil. That military cost remains modest because Readiness 3 retains the strong-army threshold. Grey Petrel was never spent and all faction assets survived. Civilian losses from the first raid are recorded as civilian costs, not sacrifices by the players who ordered it.

A public plan and a binding commitment did different jobs

The final proposal paired Relief with Restraint because it also proposed military autonomy and a Vigil Home commitment. The military article failed, so its attached Vigil pledge never activated. The army subsequently offered Vigil voluntarily and the Ascendant chose Guard. The public plan could change; the activated Wayfarer assignment could not. This distinction needs to remain explicit on the paper form.

Experience informed the final disagreement

The Janissariat delegates moved from military autonomy to joint military authorisation after seeing the value of civilian Guard and of a binding say over Spear. The Ascendant accepted lasting Guild navigation in principle, citing the delivered relief, but retained its demand for imperial military command. Guild navigation plus joint military authorisation failed 4–1 at the Ceremony, with the Ascendant opposed. Guild navigation plus imperial military command then failed 3–2, with both army players opposed. The trial Navigation article expired and both domains returned to Interim. Physical recovery and the spent trading opportunity remained.

A cost that may discourage a repeat

Venn, the Guild Navigation player, explicitly warned in debrief that paying a non-refundable commercial price for authority that expired after failed ratification would make another early commitment less attractive without greater confidence in a final deal. The trial cleared the agreed uptake test, but this is a reason to test willingness again with human players. The immediate one-round power has to be worth its price even if permanent agreement fails.

The fallback gives the crown a strong bargaining position

The Ascendant stated in debrief that failure restored its governing powers, making rejection credible despite losing the shared ratification objective. Its trial concession lasted one round; the Guild could not recover the forgone contract. This asymmetry is the next issue to examine if a lasting settlement is intended to be attractive. The reduced provisional-assent option would leave the existing final-ratification rule in place.

What to carry into the next paper playtest

Retain unanimous provisional assent, negotiate after the first result, and let a faction attach one precisely named asset assignment to an article. Make the foregone choice visible beside the pledge: Wayfarer Council means no private trading voyage that month. Keep article approval and owner consent separate, and check that the commitment actually activates.

Limits of this result

This is one fresh six-role agent run with hard-bargaining personalities, not a human playtest or a controlled comparison. Its first-round choices produced a different bargaining position from the earlier second simulation. Text exchanges were bounded and did not run against the printed 60-minute clock. Two private first-round confirmations reached their teammates verbatim only at the second-round councils; the signed assignments and public announcements had already been delivered. The facilitator records that delay. The trial does not establish that unanimity will work reliably, that both articles will be attractive, or that the full session fits its proposed timing.
