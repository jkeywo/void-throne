Rules fixed before play

The Ascendant starts with three foresight tokens. Each vision costs one; declining costs nothing. At most one vision per round. Final signature weights are Guild: 2 for intact Wayfarer + 1 for living Lantern crew + 1 for Beacon 1+; Janissariat: Readiness; Ascendant: Readiness + unspent foresight. A package needs two-thirds of all available leverage rounded up, with at least two positive-weight parties signing. A two-player faction signs only when both agree. There is no inherent final veto.

Provisional articles retain their existing assent requirements and optional named asset commitments. Surviving assets return after their service; a missed commercial voyage is an acute opportunity cost. The Ascendant seeks a dependable government that can outlast them, with no objective to retain an imperial article.

The four faction personalities remain those of the harder-bargaining trial; the Ascendant brief and personality are revised. This tests the combined changes, not a controlled isolation of voting rules from motivation. Private tabs are visible to the report reader; player access was scoped. Printed times are the proposed paper clock, not measured human play time.

The trial made a concrete difference

Guild discretion passed provisionally, with both Guild players signing a temporary Wayfarer Council commitment. Military autonomy failed because the Guild security representative refused it; the Ascendant had agreed to test it. In round two, the Guild delivered Relief and maintained the route. Both army players subsequently moved from joint Navigation to accepting Guild discretion, expressly because that service occurred. The Guild gained evidence for its claim to authority, rather than simply paying for a temporary title.

Cooperation carried costs for all three parties

An aggressive first round was followed by a cooperative recovery round. The first Raid sustained the army but left Communities at 1. In the second round, Wayfarer service displaced one commercial voyage and delivered Relief; the ship returned intact and Guild leverage remained 4. The army voluntarily stood Spear down and offered Vigil, losing one Readiness. The same loss reduced the Ascendant’s army-backed leverage by one. The Ascendant also spent personal foresight and ultimately surrendered independent command. Communities recovered to 3, Beacon stayed at 2 and Readiness ended at 3. These costs purchased useful outcomes; this was not full cooperation throughout both rounds.

The third token preserves a powerful voice

The Ascendant spent Navigation foresight in both rounds, leaving one of three tokens. That remaining token is guaranteed by the two-purchase limit; this was not deliberate hoarding. Final weights were Guild 4, Janissariat 3 and Ascendant 4: 11 available, with 8 required. Only Guild plus Ascendant could pass as a two-party coalition. Both therefore held effective blocking power at this ending, despite there being no formal personal veto. With the same physical result and two starting tokens both spent, the weights would instead be 4/3/3, requiring 7: the factions could then pass without the Ascendant. This is an arithmetic comparison, not another played simulation.

Spending and peaceful decline changed the available coalitions

After the first round, Readiness 4 and two unspent tokens gave weights 4/4/6. Buying the second vision reduced the Ascendant to 5. The peaceful second round then reduced both military-linked weights, producing 4/3/4. Had the Ascendant declined that second vision, the same physical ending would give 4/3/5 and an 8-point threshold: Ascendant plus Janissariat could still pass. The actual Ascendant explicitly preferred information to retaining those coalition options.

Ratification succeeded; reconciliation did not

The recorded approvals were Guild yes, Janissariat no, Ascendant yes. Eight supporting leverage met the threshold exactly. The same five decisions would fail under unanimous ratification. This demonstrates that weighting can produce a constitution despite continuing institutional dissent; it does not show that the dissenting army will cooperate afterwards. The army accepted Guild navigation but rejected joint military control because an outside veto could obstruct the combat needed to maintain conditioning. The Ascendant preferred joint command because ordinary Guard had helped recovery, while military autonomy’s Home duty would make that protection a breach. Future deadlock, compliance and recurring duties remain untested.

The foresight cost worked better than the foresight content

Both visions were bought before their contents or the current faction sheets were seen. Both were disclosed. The first warned about an unguarded Shared crossing; no Shared crossing was available or ordered. The second concerned Military passage and Repair plus private Trade; the actual Relief order triggered neither. No selected vision changed an operational consequence in this run. They informed precautions and confidence, and their purchase changed leverage, but this does not establish that the cards provide sufficiently useful foresight. Both Security visions remained unread. Grey Petrel was never spent.

What this run establishes—and its limits

Six distinct agents supplied the five player roles and facilitator; concurrency was limited, so independent conversations were scheduled in batches. Saved original submissions support the transcript and mechanical checks. One deliberately difficult personality mix was used. The Ascendant’s motivation and the rules both changed from the preceding trial, so the outcome cannot be attributed to voting weights alone. The final joint military article was not actually exercised provisionally: the observed Guard decision still used imperial authority, albeit with agreement from both delegates. These are evidence of willingness to cooperate and an actual successful navigation trial, not proof of a durable joint government or a timed human playtest.
