# The Void Throne — Lore Review
_Reviewed: 13 October 2026 | Scope: focused_

The prototype keeps the setting’s institutions, but changes several of the pressures that make them interesting. The largest mismatch is the Janissariat: peaceful recovery is rewarded while the cost of losing conditioned military power is absent. Fix the military dependency, faction agency and price of agreement before adjusting individual bonuses.

46 documents · 13 prototype/revision findings: 1 critical, 10 important, 2 minor · 4 separate source-version questions.

Focused review of the active v0.1 player and facilitator packs and the subsequent compromise amendment against both supplied lore projects and their shared world. The simulation is evidence about v0.1 only. The amendment is a proposal, not an implemented v0.2. This review changes no rules or previous transcript.

## How the sources were treated

The author’s instructions in this conversation take precedence over older drafts. The converted project identifies source/ as its game source and the shared Settled Dark as its fiction dependency, but is skeletal; missing details there are not evidence that the fuller legacy lore was rejected. Conflicts between source texts are recorded separately. Recommendations below are design choices, not claims that the lore already contains those rules.

The PDFs duplicate the editable packs and are not counted twice. The prototype generator was used to check exact wording and page references. The MMDL directory contains conversion guides and derived game material; it and the generic spec/ libraries were excluded from this focused lore comparison. The supplied decision-exercise and constitution-card PDFs are design inspirations, not Void Throne lore. The shared Odyssey-era file was read only to keep its separate era out of this game.

## Deliberate simplifications to retain

- Five players and one facilitator, two councils, two rounds and a 60-minute paper session. The large original cast and council structure are not requirements for this test.
- Shared faction priorities. The Guild and Janissariat may debate institutional choices together without separate character agendas or secret goals.
- No strategic map, combat subsystem, conspiracies or Clarity track. Preserve their essential causal consequences only where the small game needs them; do not restore subsystems by default.
- One imperial reserve and a choice between prepared, reliable conditional visions. These deliberately replace the larger prescience action economy. The Ascendant still should not know players’ future choices.
- Provisional articles tested in round two, followed by unanimous agreement among the played parties. Keep this structure and supply the missing wider-settlement explanation.
- Simplified public tracks, a local connected emergency and the title The Void Throne. The legacy title Throne of Ashes is a source alias, not a prototype naming mistake.

## Consistency issues

### Factual contradictions

#### 01. Military recovery replaces the pressure to fight
**Critical · score 6 · Active prototype**

**Affected:** Player pack pp. 4, 7–8 and 10; facilitator pack pp. 3–4.

Army is explicitly the stranded force’s condition, so food improving it is not itself a contradiction. The problem is that this becomes the entire military success measure: Partial rest gives Army +1, Recall +2, and autonomous Rest +2. The military article calls this responsibility for readiness. There is no separate cost for conditioning fraying, and no fighting option. The Janissariat can achieve its primary goal through peaceful recovery.

The source makes continued military effectiveness depend on maintaining conditioning. The author has reaffirmed the incentive to fight. The prototype substitutes a different institutional problem. Fraying also releases suppressed perception; it is not simply illness, cowardice or moral decline.

**Suggested direction:** Make Readiness the military power measure and print its decline without a qualifying activity. Keep supply benefits distinct in the consequences, even if they do not receive another track. Brief the faction on the tension between protecting its people and preserving its conditioned institution.

- [J-war] Factions/Janissariat High Command.md, line 5: “The Janissariat's psychic conditioning was designed for war. In peacetime, it frays. Not catastrophically — not yet. Warriors who spent decades in heightened psychic readiness are slowly becoming something more like ordinary people, and ordinary people ask questions that conditioning used to suppress.”
- [J-power] Game/Core Mechanics.md, line 67: “Janissariat unit Strength = current Readiness value (0–6). Readiness degrades each round unless maintained through external deployment or internal trials.”
- [fraying] Lore/Fraying Conditioning Thread.md, line 10: “> This is not degradation. This is the capability returning.”

### Mechanical inconsistencies

#### 02. The Ascendant does not actually depend on the army’s condition
**Important · score 4 · Active prototype**

**Affected:** Player pack pp. 4–5 and 12; facilitator pack p. 2.

Force refreshes to three every round regardless of Army. Army can reach zero without reducing the Ascendant’s powers, available Force, or ability to enforce an imperial policy. The Ascendant wants healthy tracks, but military dependence is a preference rather than a functioning rule.

In the source, high Readiness underwrites the Ascendant’s authority; low Readiness brings competing forces and commanders seeking another purpose. The author’s correction makes loyalty while fighting central. This does not establish that one battle guarantees permanent obedience or that one peaceful round causes treason.

**Suggested direction:** Print a limited authority or enforcement capability that depends on Readiness and actually matters during round two. Keep players free to negotiate their loyalty; use no automatic betrayal instruction and no additional loyalty track unless later testing proves necessary.

- [A-power] Game/Core Mechanics.md, line 62: “| 5–6 | No faction can oppose the Janissariat. The Ascendant's authority is absolute. |”
- [A-weak] Game/Core Mechanics.md, line 64: “| 1–2 | Political leverage collapses. Commanders look for purpose the Ascendant isn't providing. |”

#### 03. A constitutional prohibition prevents the physical attempt
**Important · score 4 · Active prototype**

**Affected:** Player pack pp. 2, 6, 8 and 10; facilitator pack p. 2.

Under joint military authority, Requisition is forbidden and an attempted order becomes Rest. All substantive military operations pass through the council’s current authorisation rule. The Janissariat may withhold resources but cannot initiate an unauthorised campaign or requisition. The Guild can withdraw service, but has no printed independent diversion or equivalent intervention.

The original rules give military factions their own orders, and the fiction distinguishes legal authority from power exercised outside it. More decisively, the author explicitly requires unilateral conflict with risk and reward. Removing the map and combat system need not remove that agency.

**Suggested direction:** Give each faction a small printed action using its own assets that requires no outsider’s countersignature. Separate whether it can be attempted, whether it succeeds, and whether it is lawful. Provide a reachable favourable outcome as well as a cost or failure condition.

- [own-orders] Rules.md, line 11: “Each faction meets internally. Orders are discussed, decided, and submitted to Control on the faction's Order Sheet. Payment is made on submission — currency is held by Control from the moment an order is filed.”

#### 10. The second Navigation vision has an uncovered state
**Important · score 3 · Active prototype; confirmed in simulation**

**Affected:** Facilitator pack pp. 4–5, R2 Navigation vision; simulation round-two Communion.

The low-Route branch requires opening Route 0 or 1. The high-Route branch requires opening Route 3 or 4. At opening Route 2, neither printed condition can occur that round. Later maintenance does not help because the rule explicitly uses the opening value. The simulation entered this state and selected this card.

This is a coverage defect, not a false prophecy. Reliable conditional visions are expressly required by the author and are a valid simplification of the original prescience system. The problem is that selecting this one can reveal no actionable branch.

**Suggested direction:** Print a relevant consequence for every reachable opening state, or use conditions based on available decisions rather than leaving Route 2 uncovered. Keep exact costs, triggers and effects fixed before play; retain the possibility of players averting a foreseen outcome.



### Tonal / setting drift

#### 04. The joint settlement avoids too much of the institutional price
**Important · score 2 · Active prototype and observed simulation**

**Affected:** Player pack pp. 3–5 and 9–10; version 0.1 simulation, round two and Ceremony.

Joint Navigation gives round-two Communities +2 and Army +1: the Guild’s civilian convoy gives only Communities +2. Joint Military reduces Escort to one Force. Both faction briefs accept joint authority as satisfying their constitutional priority. Deadlock and lost unilateral powers are real concessions, but the tested cooperative path achieved Route 3 / Communities 4 / Army 4 with no navigator loss. Only the Ascendant clearly surrendered a stated secondary preference.

This does not prove joint authority dominates every possible state. It does show why the cooperative test did not require the kind of compromise the author wants. The source’s military status question concerns whether the institution can be stood down or reformed, and the Guild’s question concerns protected rights—not merely who initials an order.

**Suggested direction:** Make each article commit the institution to something it otherwise wants to avoid: carriage obligations, limits on targets, oversight, lost discretion, or accepting reduced military power. Give every party a meaningful reason to bargain over the package without making cooperation automatically worse than conflict.

- [paid] game/game.md, line 25: “- grimdark stakes — every settlement is paid for”
- [J-status] Factions/Janissariat High Command.md, line 7: “The constitutional status clause in the [[The Charter of Settlement|Charter]] is not an abstraction. It is a maintenance problem dressed in legal language. A **sacred order** with autonomous doctrine cannot be stood down. An **Imperial instrument** can. The High Command is not unified on which it prefers.”
- [petition] Game/The Charter of Settlement.md, line 49: “**Pending:** Navigation Rights Petition GUILD-14, filed fourteen years ago, has not received formal response. The Guild has indicated Charter signature is contingent on resolution. The specific terms — including the scope of the phrase *associated services* — are undefined.”

### Gaps & underdeveloped areas

#### 05. The Guild has acquired an unexplained civilian-first mandate
**Important · score 2 · Active prototype assumption**

**Affected:** Player pack pp. 3 and 9.

The Guild brief values civilian supply, and Guild discretion automatically means civilian-priority cargo. The source establishes indispensable navigation, navigator protection, a petition pending for fourteen years, and ambitions concerning communications and associated services. It does not establish a general rule that Guild freedom must prioritise civilian deliveries.

Caring about civilians is plausible. Making it the mandatory meaning of Guild autonomy is an authored political position, not something inherited from the faction’s lore. It also helps make balanced joint deliveries painless to accept.

**Suggested direction:** State civilian service as a proposed Charter obligation or a specific local Guild commitment, with an explicit reason and cost. Restore a short shared demand for a response to GUILD-14 and protected navigation rights. There is no need to restore the old individual secret goals.

- [petition] Game/The Charter of Settlement.md, line 49: “**Pending:** Navigation Rights Petition GUILD-14, filed fourteen years ago, has not received formal response. The Guild has indicated Charter signature is contingent on resolution. The specific terms — including the scope of the phrase *associated services* — are undefined.”

#### 06. Route repair and military protection need a physical explanation
**Important · score 2 · Active prototype assumption**

**Affected:** Player pack pp. 1, 3, 5–6 and 11; facilitator pack pp. 3–5.

An ordinary military Escort prevents the convoy’s Route loss and both unescorted-convoy hazards, including the navigator loss. Insurance can do the same. Repair raises Route, and Bridge Navigation supplies an extra unit of navigation capacity. The pack does not establish whether these solve hostile interference, damaged infrastructure, exhausted crews, or changes in the immaterial deep.

The lore separates psychic navigability from military accessibility. Campaign Iron explicitly has navigable humanitarian routes blocked by military interdiction. The original rules give special closed-lane passage to Ductus military vessels and vessels they escort. Janissariat force alone is not established as protection against psychic failure. The current rules might be defensible, but their causal explanation is missing.

**Suggested direction:** Define what Route measures. Make military escorts protect a named physical threat, while Guild work restores a named navigational service or installation. Describe the reserve as pre-existing contracted Guild capacity or a specific prepared intervention; it must not conjure expertise. Do not make an ordinary escort cure a psychic hazard without an explicit new setting rule.

- [travel] Setting.md, line 14: “Between the inhabited worlds lies the void — not empty, but full of things that predate human ambition and will outlast it. Ships cross it in days rather than generations only because of the **[[The Ductus Guild|Ductus Guild]]**: psychic navigators who *feel* the currents of the **immaterial deep** and carry civilisation on their backs.”
- [guild-lanes] Rules.md, line 46: “**Void lanes** are managed exclusively by the Ductus Guild. Only Guild units may open or close a lane. A closed lane cannot be traversed (except by Ductus military ships or units they escort). Systems whose only connection runs through a closed lane begin losing economic output each round the lane remains closed.”
- [iron-access] Game/Fringe Campaigns.md, line 12: “The Compact's council has submitted two petitions to the Seam. The first requested reintegration terms. No response. The second requested Guild confirmation that humanitarian supply routes remain navigable. The Guild confirmed the routes are navigable. The Janissariat's interdiction makes them practically inaccessible.”

#### 07. Three signatures cannot silently replace the whole Charter’s validity
**Important · score 2 · Active prototype adaptation not explained**

**Affected:** Player pack pp. 2 and 13; facilitator pack p. 4.

The prototype calls the two-article agreement the Charter of Settlement and says it takes effect with the Guild, Janissariat and Ascendant’s signatures. The full Charter requires Ecclesiatum sanctification; the surrounding lore also gives other institutions substantive and ceremonial roles. Their absence from the player count is deliberate, but their consent has not been accounted for in the fiction.

This changes what makes government legitimate. It should not be repaired by adding a surprise facilitator veto at the Ceremony or another player role.

**Suggested direction:** Frame this as agreement on two outstanding instruments within a wider settlement. State at briefing that nonplayed institutions have already accepted the prototype’s permitted alternatives and will perform the required formalities. This is an explicit scenario adaptation, not a newly discovered canonical fact.

- [veto] Game/The Charter of Settlement.md, line 100: “This Charter shall be sanctified by the Ecclesiatum Perpetua at the Ceremony. If the Ecclesiatum withholds sanctification, the Charter does not take effect.”

#### 08. The Vethis emergency is new scenario material
**Minor · score 1 · Scenario assumption; not a contradiction**

**Affected:** Player pack pp. 1 and 7–8; Setting, Vethis Cluster and Fringe Campaigns.

A failing Vethis corridor, a stranded army, and a last scheduled convoy are the prototype’s connected crisis. The source provides the Narrow corridor, a long occupation of Vethis Secundus, and political non-cooperation, but does not establish this exact emergency. The source places Charter negotiations at the Seam; the prototype does not clearly locate the players.

The invented emergency is compatible with the setting if labelled and located. Vethis resistance is not described as a conventional enemy army. A future “fight to remain loyal” branch must not silently turn its population into convenient targets or make violence a cost-free maintenance service.

**Suggested direction:** Add a short scenario note: the players meet at the Seam; identify the threatened link and which force is stranded; state the immediate cause and the local population’s demand. If using the Narrow, identify that choice as this prototype’s scenario. Give any proposed combat target its own concrete situation and consequences.



#### 09. Real-time rounds have no stated fictional duration or scale
**Important · score 2 · Active prototype gap**

**Affected:** Player pack pp. 1, 7–8 and 12; facilitator runsheet.

The schedule specifies minutes at the table, but not how much fictional time a round represents. Orders produce deliveries, repairs and recovery before a Ceremony presented as happening today. The tokens also alternate between capacity and the loss of “a navigator,” without locating the three-token pool in a specific local operation.

Source ships cross the void in days; campaigns and conditioning changes develop over longer periods. A small corridor command can plausibly have three units of capacity, but these are not the entire empire’s three navigators or its whole army. The mismatch is undefined compression, not a confirmed timeline contradiction.

**Suggested direction:** Choose and state the frame: for example, rounds represent successive operational periods before the final Ceremony, with table minutes compressing that time. Define tokens as this corridor’s available capacity and describe what losing one represents. If retaining a literal single Charter Day, explain why the resolved effects are already-in-motion events or forecasts rather than completed new journeys.

- [travel] Setting.md, line 14: “Between the inhabited worlds lies the void — not empty, but full of things that predate human ambition and will outlast it. Ships cross it in days rather than generations only because of the **[[The Ductus Guild|Ductus Guild]]**: psychic navigators who *feel* the currents of the **immaterial deep** and carry civilisation on their backs.”

#### 11. Combat-only maintenance is a new interpretation, not the old rule
**Important · score 2 · Recent amendment requires a defined adaptation**

**Affected:** Compromise revision, “Replacement military choices” and core rule.

The amendment says a round without qualifying combat reduces Readiness and an evaded target does not count. The original mechanics allow maintenance through external deployment or internal trials. Fringe Campaigns says properly resourced campaigns generate Readiness; Campaign Ash has only three confirmed engagements in seven months.

The author’s correction supports fighting as a central incentive and takes precedence over the older draft. It still leaves the exact qualifying activity and timescale undefined. My amendment narrowed the existing rule without presenting that narrowing clearly enough. No source establishes an exact casualty cost for trials or a universal amount restored per battle.

**Suggested direction:** Define which printed operations sustain conditioning in this small game, and which merely consume resources. If requiring actual contact, mark that as this prototype’s interpretation of the author’s correction. Either omit internal trials explicitly or give them a chosen, printed tradeoff; do not present an invented cost as established lore.

- [J-power] Game/Core Mechanics.md, line 67: “Janissariat unit Strength = current Readiness value (0–6). Readiness degrades each round unless maintained through external deployment or internal trials.”
- [campaign] Game/Fringe Campaigns.md, line 1: “Three active campaigns at the edge of the navigable dark. Each generates [[Core Mechanics#The Readiness Track|Readiness]] when properly resourced. Each generates political consequences when news of its conduct reaches the Seam.”
- [ash] Game/Fringe Campaigns.md, line 23: “Three confirmed engagements have occurred in seven months.”

#### 12. Veteran transition is plausible; quick navigator conversion is not established
**Important · score 2 · Recent amendment: speculative proposal**

**Affected:** Compromise revision, “A peaceful compromise with a cost for all three.”

The amendment proposes a Guild-sponsored transition programme based on returning perceptual capacity. It already says veterans are not trained navigators and the programme is not a cure. However, it does not mention that Guild candidates spend thirty to seventy years learning, nor establish that veterans can be trained safely or successfully. Joining Guild training also does not inherently end a Guild monopoly: access and licensing terms matter.

A promise to investigate and support veterans can be a credible long-term bargain. It cannot justify new Navigator tokens, immediate replacement of lost specialists, guaranteed rehabilitation, or an automatic end to exclusive Guild control during these two rounds.

**Suggested direction:** If retained, make this a funded assessment and transition commitment whose outcome remains uncertain. Print the present sacrifice—staff time, resources, admission rights, or shared oversight—and distinguish those from later hoped-for benefits. Any surrender of monopoly must be an express right granted by the article. This is an optional design addition, not a required lore repair.

- [training] Systems/Outer Meridian Arm.md, line 40: “Where navigators are made. Candidates spend thirty to seventy years learning to translate what they feel into something ships can follow. The training process pushes sensitivity deliberately. The washout rate is not publicly disclosed.”
- [fraying] Lore/Fraying Conditioning Thread.md, line 10: “> This is not degradation. This is the capability returning.”

#### 13. A vision revealing the whole veteran mechanism would relocate hidden knowledge
**Minor · score 1 · Recent amendment: optional information change**

**Affected:** Compromise revision, transition proposal; Fraying Conditioning Thread.

The amendment suggests foresight might disclose the revelation about veterans’ perceptions. In the source, the full connection requires Ae2’s research and J3’s operational records, and nobody currently possesses both. The Ascendant is not established as already understanding the mechanism.

Reliable foresight about consequences does not automatically confer a scientific explanation or a guarantee that a transition programme works. The five-player adaptation may redistribute information, but should do so deliberately. Shared faction goals do not require restoring individual secrets.

**Suggested direction:** Prefer a vision of a specific observable conditional outcome. If the full explanation is needed, move the relevant evidence into a shared faction brief or facilitator report and label that redistribution as a prototype adaptation. Keep the deeper cosmic explanation optional.

- [knowledge] Lore/Fraying Conditioning Thread.md, line 3: “> *[[Ae2]]'s research, the three [[Irongate]] veterans, the [[Vigil Prime]] verification failures, and the [[Vethis Secundus]] underground event are connected. The path toward this conclusion requires someone with access to both Ae2's research and J3's operational records. No one currently has both.*”

## Source-version questions

These four differences are not additional defects attributed to the prototype. They need an explicit editorial choice if the relevant material is reused.

### S1. What is the Ascendant trying to preserve?

The legacy Ascendant brief seeks a workable Charter, healthy pillars and resolution without giving one faction disproportionate advantage. The converted objective demands authority “intact and unshared,” while its own threshold says the Charter is signed at all. These imply different acceptable compromises. The author’s latest better-future/needs-the-army direction should govern this prototype; do not silently treat either older objective as the sole canon.

**Sources:** The Ascendant.md, Goals; source/game/factions.gamespec.md, seal_the_peace and ascendant_brief.

- [unshared] game/factions.gamespec.md, line 50: “statement: The Charter is signed today with the Ascendant's authority intact and unshared.”

### S2. Has the sanctification veto already been conceded?

The legacy Charter says it is already an Ascendant concession; the shared Settlement-era world and the converted Ecclesiatum description also recognise the veto. The converted Ascendant ambition is to avoid ceding it. This could require a clarified draft stage, but none is specified. Record the difference rather than pretending all sources agree.

**Sources:** Game/The Charter of Settlement.md §7.2; shared entities/settlement_era.md; source/game/factions.gamespec.md.

- [veto] Game/The Charter of Settlement.md, line 100: “This Charter shall be sanctified by the Ecclesiatum Perpetua at the Ceremony. If the Ecclesiatum withholds sanctification, the Charter does not take effect.”
- [ceding] game/factions.gamespec.md, line 66: “statement: Ambition — it is signed without ceding the sanctification veto to the Ecclesiatum.”

### S3. Which original crisis system is intended?

Legacy Five Pillars/Crisis Events trigger a corresponding crisis when a pillar reaches one. The converted council source uses a phase-based sequence of four crises. These are different game structures. The user has authorised a new fixed two-round situation, so neither old system needs to be reinstated.

**Sources:** Game/The Five Pillars.md; Game/Crisis Events.md; source/game/councils.gamespec.md.



### S4. When was the conquest completed?

The legacy setting describes the Conclave being broken world by world from sixty years ago and its last fleet defeated eleven months ago. The converted premise says “Sixty years after” the Ascendant broke the Conclave; the shared chronology describes a sixty-year conquest. This is an ambiguity in the historical shorthand rather than proof of an impossible sequence. Use an explicit start, final victory and Charter date before printing a timeline.

**Sources:** Setting.md; source/game/game.md, premise; shared chronology/eras.md.

- [date] Setting.md, line 22: “Sixty years ago, the [[The Ascendant|Ascendant]] broke the Conclave, world by world. The [[Janissariat High Command|Janissariat]] — three billion psychically conditioned warriors — made it possible. Eleven months ago, the last Conclave loyalist fleet broke at the [[Pale Meridian|Battle of Pale Meridian]]. The conquest is complete.”
- [new-date] game/game.md, line 18: “Sixty years after the Ascendant broke the Conclave of Thrones, the conquest is complete”

## Recommended revision order

1. Define Readiness, qualifying military activity and the Ascendant’s practical dependence on it.
2. Give unilateral operations a printed attempt, success condition and cost; distinguish law from physical capability.
3. Rewrite the six articles and shared priorities so each cooperative package requires identifiable concessions.
4. Explain navigation hazards, the local scenario, time compression and wider Charter consent.
5. Replace the uncovered vision and decide whether the optional veteran programme belongs in this small test.
6. Update both packs together, then simulate the revised rules. Do not treat the previous simulation as evidence that the revision works.
