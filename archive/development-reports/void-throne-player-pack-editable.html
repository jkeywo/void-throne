PLAYER GUIDE | 1 OF 2

The conquest is complete. The peace has not begun. An army waits beyond the failing Vethis corridor. The settlements along its supply route are running short. Today you must keep them alive and decide who will govern tomorrow.

A paper megagame prototype by John Keyworth, developed with Codex.
Version 0.1 | 13 October 2026 | Five players + one facilitator | 60 minutes

Your place in the empire

Two players form the Ductus Guild; two form the Janissariat. Agree your faction decisions and priorities together. You have no individual secret goals. Each faction sends one member to each council. The fifth player is the Ascendant, the sovereign who can see possible futures.

What you are trying to achieve

Keep the empire functioning while securing your faction's future. Three public tracks run from 0 to 4: Route (corridor reliability), Communities (civilian supply and confidence), and Army (the stranded force's condition). All start at 2. Higher is better, but your priorities differ.

Navigator and Force tokens are capacity for the current round, not points. Only committed tokens may pay for council orders. There is no trading between token types, no borrowing, no income calculation and no map movement. Unused capacity does not carry over.

Some consequences are unknown until an order resolves. They are fixed in the facilitator pack. Foresight can reveal some of them. No dice are rolled.

Player guide pp. 1-2; shared briefs pp. 3-5; council references p. 6; decision cards pp. 7-8; Charter articles pp. 9-10; Ascendant slips p. 11; public board and tokens p. 12; settlement sheet p. 13. The facilitator holds a separate pack containing spoilers.

How decisions become consequences

PLAYER GUIDE | 2 OF 2

1. Faction phase - five minutes

Read this round's situation on the council cards. Each faction circles one option on its faction card, both members sign, and places committed tokens in its named public pool. Commitments lock at the bell. You cannot add, withdraw or transfer them later. If your two members cannot agree or miss the deadline, use Maintenance for the Guild and Partial rest for the Janissariat. The facilitator applies those defaults.

The Ascendant has a private Communion: select one of the two visions for this round, keep that card, and optionally lodge a reserve intervention. No selection by the faction bell means no vision that round. The intervention also locks at that bell. Visions may be shown or discussed, but taking the other vision is not permitted.

2. Council phase - seven minutes

Move to your assigned council. Choose an operational order using the authority in force at the start of this round. Record it on the council card. An imperial directive is binding only where imperial authority currently applies. You may send written notes between tables; remain at your own council. The Ascendant may revise a lawful directive until the bell; the final entry on the council card is authoritative.

The person or people with authority initial that entry. A joint decision requires both faction delegates' initials; no agreement means Close at Navigation or Rest at Security. Missing, multiple, forbidden or incorrectly authorised orders use the same default. No extra penalty applies solely for a missing or invalid order.

In round one, also propose one Charter article per council. Both delegates and the Ascendant must sign the proposal fields by the council bell. Consent is separate from the operational order. If an article is not validly agreed, its category retains the interim rule. Round-two articles cannot change during the round.

3. Free discussion - six minutes

The facilitator first resolves the locked orders, announces changed tracks and any triggered consequences, and posts articles for round two. Use the remaining time for audiences and negotiations. These discussions create promises, not new actions, resources or immediate amendments. Faction cards lock again next round.

Paying for orders

A paid order runs only if its committed pool can meet the full cost. A reserve intervention can sometimes cover a one-token shortfall. Otherwise it becomes Close or Rest, no tokens are spent on it, and Communities falls by 1 for each unfunded paid order. Both councils resolve together: their effects can support or harm each other.

All track changes are added together, then each track is limited to 0-4 once, at the end of the round. A track reaching 0 does not eliminate anyone or cancel the Ceremony.

The Ceremony

Choose one Navigation article and one Military article. Both members must authorise their faction's signature. The Guild, Janissariat and Ascendant must all sign the same completed settlement sheet. Any combination of the six printed articles is compatible. No handwritten rule changes or side deals alter the printed mechanics.

If ratified, those articles define the empire's future. If agreement fails, all provisional articles expire and the interim arrangements return. The tracks and losses remain as played. There is no extra action round or automatic recovery.

Ductus Guild

SHARED FACTION BRIEF | TWO PLAYERS

We carried this empire before it had a name. Orders do not steer ships. Navigators do.

The Vethis corridor is failing. You can keep traffic moving, but each rushed passage leaves less time to maintain the route. The Ascendant sees possible futures. You must decide whether to trust those warnings with the lives of your people.

Your shared priorities

First: end with Route at 3 or 4 and no navigator lost.
Second: secure a ratified Navigation article with Guild discretion or joint authorisation.
Also: keep Communities at 2 or more. A safe route that serves nobody is a poor defence of your monopoly.

Starting assetsUse
3 Navigator tokens each roundCommit these to the Navigation pool. If a navigator is lost in round one, round two has only 2.
One seat on each councilDecide who attends which council now. Keep those seats for both rounds.

Your faction decision

Full service: commit every available Navigator. No maintenance bonus.
Maintenance: keep 2 Navigators at home; commit the remainder. Route +1.
Withdraw: commit none. Route +2; Communities -1.

With 3 available tokens, those commitments are 3, 1 and 0. With 2, they are 2, 0 and 0. Maintenance and withdrawal have different effects even when both commit zero. Effects apply during resolution, not when you choose.

At the councils

Navigation decides whether the corridor carries a convoy, receives repairs, or closes. Security decides whether the army escorts traffic, requisitions local stores, or rests. An escort can protect the route during a convoy. Your security delegate can therefore protect Guild interests without commanding a single soldier.

Your constitutional bargain

N-G: Guild discretion gives your navigation delegate the decision and gives civilians priority in convoys.
N-I: Imperial priority puts the Ascendant in charge and directs convoy benefits to the army.
N-J: Joint authorisation requires both delegates' consent and keeps balanced deliveries.

You may accept military authority you dislike to secure the navigation settlement you need. Consider whether a cheaper joint escort is worth giving the other faction a voice over your operations.

Practical guidance

Agree what you can offer before separating. Ask what the Ascendant actually saw, including the condition attached to the vision. A warning is not a command. Explain shortages before a directive becomes an unfunded order. Keep this brief available to both faction members.

Janissariat

SHARED FACTION BRIEF | TWO PLAYERS

We won the war. We would like our soldiers to survive the peace.

Your stranded force needs supplies, rest and a route home. It can escort traffic, but those escorts come from the same exhausted troops. Requisitioning civilian stores will sustain the army quickly. The settlements may remember how you did it.

Your shared priorities

First: end with Army at 3 or 4.
Second: secure a ratified Military article with military autonomy or joint authorisation.
Also: keep Communities at 2 or more. An army surrounded by hostile settlements has not secured peace.

Starting assetsUse
3 Force tokens each roundCommit these to the Security pool. There are no permanent Force losses in this prototype.
One seat on each councilDecide who attends which council now. Keep those seats for both rounds.

Your faction decision

Mobilise: commit all 3 Force. No rest bonus.
Partial rest: commit 1 Force. Army +1.
Recall: commit none. Army +2; Communities -1.

These are choices about your own assets. An imperial order cannot change your locked commitment. Effects apply during resolution. A later council order may still be unfunded if the Ascendant demands more than you supplied.

At the councils

Navigation decides what crosses the corridor. Your delegate can bargain for army deliveries, or support repairs now in exchange for later relief. Security chooses Escort, Requisition or Rest. Escort normally costs 2 Force; Requisition costs 3; Rest is free.

Escort protects a funded convoy from the printed Route loss. Without a convoy it spends its cost but provides no ordinary benefit. Requisition gives Army +2 and Communities -1. Rest gives Army +1 and Communities -1, with a different Army benefit under military autonomy.

Your constitutional bargain

M-I: Imperial command lets the Ascendant choose and grants Army +1 when your funded Escort accompanies a funded convoy.
M-A: Military autonomy lets your security delegate choose; Rest gives Army +2 instead of +1.
M-J: Joint authorisation makes Escort cost only 1 Force, but prohibits Requisition and requires both delegates' consent.

Practical guidance

Military autonomy is useful only if the resulting empire can supply you. The Guild controls the transport you need. Your faction can coordinate its council positions; you do not need to undermine your teammate. Promises about next round are political commitments, not enforceable resource transfers.

The Ascendant

SOLE PLAYER BRIEF | KEEP VISIONS PRIVATE UNTIL YOU CHOOSE TO SHARE

I have seen an empire that survives me. I have also seen several that do not.

You broke the old order. Now the people who supplied your conquest want to decide how you may govern. You can see consequences hidden from them, but you cannot see what they will choose. You have enough authority to issue orders and too few assets to execute them alone.

Your priorities

First: ratify a Charter and end with all three tracks at 2 or more.
Second: retain at least one imperial article, N-I or M-I, without sacrificing that workable peace.
Legacy: explain one disaster you prevented, or one power you surrendered for a reason.

Communion

During each faction phase, choose Navigation or Security and receive only that round's matching vision. The facilitator explains any terms but does not reveal the other card. Read the exact conditions. Keep, describe or show the card as you choose. It is permissible to conceal it or misrepresent what you saw; the facilitator will not certify your claim to other players.

Every vision is true if its printed conditions occur. Players and your reserve may prevent those conditions. The visions do not reveal players' intentions, guarantee an outcome or change a submitted decision. No further visions can be purchased.

Your reserve

You have one Reserve token for the whole game. Before the council phase, use the intervention sheet to choose Insurance, Bridge Navigation, Bridge Security, or Keep. Place the token on a lodged intervention. You cannot revise it during councils. An untriggered intervention expires and returns the token; a triggered intervention spends it permanently.

Insurance: if a funded convoy has no funded military Escort, provide protection against convoy Route loss and hazards that require an unescorted convoy. It supplies no other military, constitutional or vision bonus.
Bridge: cover exactly a one-token shortfall in the named council's selected legal paid order. It cannot fund a larger gap or both councils.

Your authority

In round one, you select both councils' operational orders under the interim rules. You may leave a public directive, change it in writing before the bell, or accept a council's recommendation by initialling its order card. An order outside your current authority is only advice.

In round two, read the posted articles. You choose Navigation under N-I or the interim rule; Security under M-I or the interim rule. Other articles transfer those decisions. Your assent is required to enact a provisional article and your signature is required on the final Charter. You cannot unilaterally revoke a provisional article during round two.

Where you play

Faction phase: Communion with the facilitator. Council phase: Security and Government, with written notes to Navigation. Free discussion: move freely and hold audiences. Keep your orders legible; the final council card, not an earlier note or spoken instruction, records the order.

Navigation articles

CUT INTO THREE | PUBLIC CHARTER OPTIONS

N-G | Guild discretion

Power: the Guild navigation delegate selects Convoy, Repair or Close. The Ascendant cannot countermand that choice.

Obligation: convoy space prioritises civilian deliveries. A funded Convoy gives Communities +2 and no Army cargo benefit, replacing the normal cargo effects. Its Route loss and protection rules still apply.

Resources: Convoy costs 2 Navigators; Repair 2; Close 0. Only committed capacity can be spent. Owning the authority does not waive a cost.

Settlement meaning: navigation is a protected Guild service with a civilian supply duty. It does not control military deployments.

N-I | Imperial priority

Power: the Ascendant selects Convoy, Repair or Close in writing. The Guild retains control of its faction commitment.

Obligation: convoy space prioritises the stranded army. A funded Convoy gives Army +2 and no Communities cargo benefit, replacing the normal cargo effects. Its Route loss and protection rules still apply.

Resources: Convoy costs 2 Navigators; Repair 2; Close 0. If committed capacity is insufficient, the order fails normally. The sovereign cannot take uncommitted Navigators.

Settlement meaning: the crown sets transport priorities but must obtain Guild capacity to carry them out.

N-J | Joint authorisation

Power: both Navigation delegates must initial the same order. Neither faction nor the Ascendant can decide alone. If they disagree or miss the deadline, use Close.

Obligation: convoys divide capacity between army and settlements. In round two a funded Convoy gives Communities +2 and Army +1. Its Route loss and protection rules still apply.

Resources: Convoy costs 2 Navigators; Repair 2; Close 0. No additional tax or token is required for joint consent.

Settlement meaning: neither institution controls passage alone. Both accept balanced deliveries and the risk of deadlock.

THE VOID THRONE / 0.1

Military articles

CUT INTO THREE | PUBLIC CHARTER OPTIONS

M-I | Imperial command

Power: the Ascendant selects Escort, Requisition or Rest. The Janissariat retains control of its faction commitment.

Obligation: central coordination gives Army +1 whenever a funded military Escort accompanies a funded Convoy, in addition to all cargo effects. This applies under any Navigation article. Reserve Insurance alone does not earn it.

Resources: Escort costs 2 Force; Requisition 3; Rest 0. Rest gives Army +1 and Communities -1. Insufficient capacity still causes normal failure.

Settlement meaning: the military remains an imperial instrument, with responsibility for coordinated army supply.

M-A | Military autonomy

Power: the Janissariat security delegate selects Escort, Requisition or Rest. The Ascendant cannot countermand that choice.

Obligation: military command must prioritise recovery when resting. Rest gives Army +2 instead of +1, and still gives Communities -1. It receives no M-I convoy bonus.

Resources: Escort costs 2 Force; Requisition 3; Rest 0. Only committed Force is available. Autonomy does not supply extra tokens.

Settlement meaning: the Janissariat is an autonomous order responsible for its own readiness; civilian supply is not automatically its first priority.

M-J | Joint authorisation

Power: both Security delegates must initial the same order. If they disagree or miss the deadline, use Rest. The Ascendant has no casting vote.

Obligation: Requisition is forbidden. Civilian cooperation makes Escort cost 1 Force instead of 2. Rest remains Army +1 and Communities -1. There is no M-I convoy bonus.

Resources: Escort 1 Force; Rest 0. A Requisition instruction is invalid and defaults to Rest without an unfunded-order penalty.

Settlement meaning: deployments need institutional consent. Local assistance lowers escort costs, in exchange for protection against military requisition.

If ratified

If not ratified
