A playable revision, ready for another test.

Five players, one facilitator, two rounds and a final settlement. All gameplay is on paper. Costs, conditions, duties, defaults, deadlines and consequences are printed. The previous simulation describes v0.1; this revision has had mechanical desk checks, not a new agent simulation or human playtest.

What can be committed

Wayfarer

Guild freight vessel

Offer it to Navigation, or carry out a commercial voyage.

The cost of committing it
Relief or military passage costs a commercial opportunity. A poorly protected private voyage can disable the ship.

Lantern

Guild survey tender

Maintain the beacon independently, or support a council Repair/Shared voyage.

The cost of committing it
The same crew cannot maintain the installation and accompany the convoy. A printed physical threat can remove the navigation team.

Spear

9th Pale Cohort

Offer it to Security, launch an independent Raid, or Stand down.

The cost of committing it
Fighting can sustain conditioning at a civilian cost. Missed contact lets conditioning decline; weak forces risk being disabled.

Vigil

Narrow garrison

Offer it for Guard/Warrant, or keep it at the order’s bases.

The cost of committing it
The crown needs offered troops and sufficient Readiness for its Warrant. Autonomy requires keeping this garrison Home or accepting a breach.

Grey Petrel

Ascendant’s contracted reserve cutter

Pre-commit one conditional Relief lift or Tender cover.

The cost of committing it
One intervention in the whole prototype. It cannot lift the cohort, mend the beacon or perform both tasks.

The Narrow is the shared route. Its physical beacon has a condition track; it is not a portable token. The Charter determines who chooses its traffic. The ships and formations are the things players actually commit. This keeps a route, a ship and an army distinct without adding a map.

Why this is workable on paper

Each faction has only two asset cards. An asset has one assignment per round. The card moves beside its assignment, then returns if it survives. If Wayfarer is disabled, the next round is visibly missing a ship; players do not subtract a generic transport point and forget what happened.

Commercial contracts are obligations recorded on the public sheet, not another spendable currency. Beacon, Communities and Readiness are condition tracks. Keeping those few aggregate measures avoids giving every vessel its own fuel, crew, damage and supply accounts.

The limit is the number of exceptions. This version already needs a resolution sequence for protection, passage, target presence and losses. Keep the four faction assets for the next test. Add another only if players need a distinct choice that none of these can express.

Cooperation now has a price

PartyIn the worked cooperative settlement
GuildForgoes both commercial contracts, ends with a strained beacon, and accepts shared traffic authority.
JanissariatAccepts one peaceful round, losing a point of conditioned power, and allows the Guild a veto over lawful operations.
AscendantShares both operational powers and loses the independent imperial Warrant, while retaining a capable army.
Worked result: Beacon 1 / Communities 4 / Readiness 3. All assets survive; no commercial contracts are completed. The reserve remains unused. This is survival with concessions, not an assertion that this is the best possible bargain.

A different cooperative agreement can support campaigning in both rounds and retain Readiness 4. That still costs Guild commercial work, military independence under M-I, and the Ascendant’s exclusive control of passage under N-G. Cooperation need not always mean peace; the constitutional concessions must still matter.

Conflict is a physical option

Spear can attempt an independent Raid without consent from the council, crown or Guild. Wayfarer can sail on a private commercial voyage. The Charter can make either decision a breach, but it cannot erase the submitted action. Target presence, route preparation, protection and Readiness determine the outcome.

Checked contrast, with spoilers: in round one, a completed private commercial voyage draws the remnant detachment out. A strong independent Raid can fight it, preserving military power at a severe civilian and political cost. Change the Guild’s decision to Relief and that same Raid misses its target. There is a reachable reward and a reachable disappointment.

Editorial choices between source versions

  • The Ascendant seeks a workable future and a dependable army; unshared authority is a preference they can sacrifice, not a mandatory victory condition.
  • Ecclesiatum sanctification remains required in the fiction. Its consent to these six permitted alternatives is secured before the scenario.
  • The author’s two-round structure replaces both older crisis systems. Neither the pillar-triggered crises nor the converted phase clock is reinstated.
  • The conquest has ended and the settlement remains unresolved. The prototype avoids the ambiguous “sixty years after” shorthand and gives its own operations an explicit two-month timescale.

These are choices for this prototype. The source lore repositories have not been edited. Wayfarer, Lantern, Spear, Vigil, Grey Petrel, their local duties and this crisis are scenario additions, not claims about names already present in the original lore.

Checks completed

26 named assertions and 46,080 combinations of single-round choices passed. The combination checks cover bounded track values and Readiness behaviour; the named cases check cross-round cooperation, unilateral risk/reward, unavailable resources, averted prophecy, reserve limits, conflicting asset claims and failed ratification.

Both PDFs were rendered and visually inspected: 22 pages in total. This establishes that the printed materials are complete and the checked consequences agree. It does not establish balance, enjoyment or that resolution fits comfortably inside the free-discussion phase.
