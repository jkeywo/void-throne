The Void Throne · Foresight revision

Running the revised visions

Facilitator only. The following pages include all four private answers. Give players only the player update and this round’s public covers.

Before play

Use the v0.2 physical rules and weighted-settlement experiment. Replace the four old vision cards with these four pairs. Their consequences are unchanged. Cut the cover and answer panels apart. Keep each answer in an opaque envelope labelled with its matching cover. Prepare three foresight tokens and the separate reserve coin.

During each faction phase

  1. At minute 2, display the factions’ draft slips and the Ascendant’s intended directives. Display both current-round covers every time, regardless of which conditions will eventually occur. Never select a cover as a hint.
  2. Explain ordinary rules freely. Do not confirm a hidden trigger, reveal an answer or calculate a hidden outcome before purchase. Missing drafts stay UNDECLARED; do not supply an intention on a player’s behalf.
  3. At minute 3, record exactly one card ID or NONE. Remove one token before privately giving the selected answer. Invalid or late selection means NONE, cost zero. Publish only expenditure and remaining tokens; recalculate public leverage.
  4. Allow the final two minutes for disclosure and revisions. At minute 5, collect actual signed faction sheets and lodge the reserve. Enforce existing Standing Commitments. Buying information does not reopen a locked pledge.
  5. Run councils normally. No later purchase or reserve reassignment. Resolve final authorised orders, including any changed plans and unilateral actions, against every hidden consequence whether read or unread.

When someone asks “What happens if…?”

Point to the purchased card’s printed branch and the public rules. Do not invent another vision. The conditional answer remains true if the proposal changes, but no longer describes that plan if its conditions no longer hold. An unused warning receives no refund.

Do not force a purchase. If the proposed operations fall outside both covers, conserving foresight is a successful use of the choice. A player may still buy information about a possible change of plan.

This replaces the old instruction to select foresight before seeing other factions’ plans. Reserve still locks at the faction bell; orders and Warrant at the council bell. Faction and council defaults, article powers, reserve triggers and final voting weights are unchanged.

Private answer · R1-N

The exposed transfer

Shared crosses without protection. If funded Shared passage is delivered and there is no funded Guard or valid Warrant, Lantern’s navigation team is lost. A lodged, available Grey Petrel on Tender cover prevents this loss and is spent.

Shared crosses with protection. A funded Guard or valid Warrant prevents this crew loss. Tender cover does not trigger and stays available.

Shared does not cross. This crew-loss effect does not occur. An unfunded or halted order still has its normal cost.

Apply the public rules

Shared needs both Wayfarer and Lantern on Council. Passage still needs prepared Beacon 2+. Guard needs offered Vigil; a Warrant also needs imperial authority and opening Readiness 3+. Protection does not repair Beacon or avoid crossing wear.

Use the information: Compare Shared with and without available protection. Buying this card does not predict whether either faction will supply it.

Private answer · R1-S

The merchant’s wake

Wayfarer completes private Trade. A Raid finds the target. At opening Readiness 2+, it fights: total Raid cost Communities −2 and the round’s combat change is Readiness +1. At Readiness 0–1, Spear is disabled: Raid costs Communities −1 and there is no qualifying combat from that Raid.

No completed private Trade. The Raid finds no target. It costs Communities −1, Spear remains intact, and it provides no qualifying combat. Council Relief, Military or Shared passage does not substitute for a private contract.

No Raid. These Raid effects do not occur. Campaign and other orders follow their own rules.

Apply the public rules

A Trade assignment is not enough: the voyage must complete under the public Beacon and protection rules. If no operation achieves combat, Readiness falls by 1 once. Duty breaches and monthly consumption are additional; tracks stay within 0–4.

Use the information: Compare Raid with a known noncombat option, or check whether the proposed private voyage would actually complete. The card does not make combat harmless.

Stores at the crossing

Military passage crosses unguarded. Delivered Military passage without funded Guard or valid Warrant causes an extra Communities −1. Protection prevents this extra loss. Grey Petrel’s Tender cover does not protect these stores.

Funded Repair and completed Trade. A funded council Repair together with a completed private Wayfarer Trade gives Communities +1 from distribution. Both must happen. Lantern must be on Council for Repair; Maintain is not Repair.

Neither condition. Neither extra supply effect applies. Relief and Shared receive their normal cargo effects; other cards and ordinary costs still apply.

Use the information: Compare Military with or without protection, or Repair/Trade against another use of the ships. This card provides no new complication for a Relief/Guard plan.

The relief column

Relief is delivered. A Raid finds the target if the authorised, funded Navigation order is Relief and its voyage actually crosses. Grey Petrel Relief lift counts. At opening Readiness 2+, Raid costs Communities −2 total and achieves combat; at 0–1, Spear is disabled, Raid costs Communities −1 and provides no qualifying combat.

Relief is not delivered. The Raid finds no target, costs Communities −1, leaves Spear intact and provides no qualifying combat. A proposed, unfunded or halted Relief order does not qualify. Shared, Military and private Trade do not qualify.

No Raid. Relief alone does not cause an attack or a Raid penalty. These effects require an actual Raid assignment or funded council Raid.

Apply the public rules

Combat changes Readiness by +1 once; no successful combat changes it by −1 once. Ordinary cargo, Guard, duty breaches and consumption still apply. A council Raid needs offered Spear; an independent Raid uses its own assignment.

Use the information: Compare Raid with restraint or protection. Relief can enable a Raid, but does not force the army to make one; foresight cannot reveal its future choice.

What the revision changes

Revisit the last simulation

Actual situationWhat the new procedure offers
Round one: Trade/Maintain proposed; council Raid being considered.The Raid cover advertises target information. Buying R1-S reveals that a completed private contract brings the target within reach. The Ascendant can weigh that against the known civilian cost, request a different assignment, or decline the vision and accept uncertainty.
Round two: Wayfarer pledged to Council; Relief/Guard proposed, Spear stood down.R2-N concerns Military or Repair/Trade. R2-S concerns Raid. Neither provides a hidden benefit or penalty for that unchanged plan. NONE preserves leverage. R2-S could still be worth buying to understand a possible reversal towards Raid.

These are design checks using actual past proposals and outcomes. They do not claim what the agents would choose under the revised procedure. Earlier transcripts remain historical records.

The choice still costs something

Information arrives early enough to change unpledged assignments. It can identify a useful combat opportunity or a preventable loss. It does not cancel the civilian cost of violence, restore Readiness after peace, supply an unavailable asset or guarantee compliance.

At the previous ending, spending twice gave final weights Guild 4 / Janissariat 3 / Ascendant 4, threshold 8. Buying once and declining once would instead give 4 / 3 / 5, also threshold 8, if physical play stayed the same. That would preserve an Ascendant–Janissariat winning coalition. Actual bargains and operations could change.

Check in the next playtest

  1. Before purchase, record the proposed action or possible reversal that makes the chosen card relevant.
  2. After reading, record one changed assignment, order, protection measure or bargaining claim. If none changed, ask whether the information increased confidence.
  3. When choosing NONE, record whether the reason was irrelevance, confidence in others, retaining leverage or knowingly accepting a risk.
  4. Check whether the extra draft-sharing step fits the five-minute faction phase and preserves useful negotiation time.
Branch checks passed. Shared protection and halted passage; Raid target present, absent and low-Readiness branches; guarded Military loading; Repair/Trade distribution; Grey Petrel Relief; and both physical outcomes from the last run. No new six-agent simulation has been run for this revision.

Design trade-off: draft intentions now become public before commitments. Players may change or withhold an intention, but cannot conceal a binding final assignment once the faction bell arrives. Cards remain fixed and reliable; there is no improvised prophecy adjudication and no digital tool is needed during play.
