THE VOID THRONE · REVISION DRAFT · 13 OCTOBER 2026

The army needs a war

Design amendment for discussion and the next prototype. This records the revised incentives and replacement rule direction. It does not replace the existing player and facilitator packs yet; their costs, articles and consequence tables still describe version 0.1. The previous simulation remains a record of that version.

The conquest is complete. The force that won it cannot safely be left in peace. The Ascendant needs that force to build something better than another conquest.

The correction

The prototype treated Army as a supply-and-recovery track. Partial Rest, Rest and convoy deliveries could all increase it. That removed the Janissariat's central pressure: psychic conditioning frays without fighting. The setting already connects declining Readiness with commanders seeking a purpose the Ascendant is not providing. The author's clarification adds the essential dependency: the Ascendant has their loyalty while they fight, and needs their army.

Use Readiness to mean conditioned combat power. Food keeps troops supplied; it does not replace the fighting that sustains their conditioning. A peaceful outcome can therefore be good for the empire while weakening both the Janissariat and the Ascendant.

What each party must compromise

PartyWhat it valuesWhat a negotiated settlement asks it to give up
Ductus GuildControl of passage, navigator safety and an exclusive navigation service.A binding military transport obligation and some discretion over who travels. Supporting even a limited campaign consumes scarce passages that could serve communities or maintain routes.
JanissariatConditioned strength, institutional survival and the freedom to find necessary fighting.Restraint over targets, civilian scrutiny and a real possibility of declining Readiness when permission or transport is unavailable. A charter cannot promise both permanent restraint and unchanged combat power for free.
AscendantA better empire, a capable army and the loyalty on which sovereign authority depends.Either accept a less dependable military foundation for peace, or accept continuing violence and share control of its targets and transport with the other institutions.

These are shared faction priorities. Do not reintroduce separate secret goals for teammates. Do not make one party the automatic sacrifice while everyone else keeps their ideal outcome. A successful bargain should include an identifiable concession by each party.

A peaceful compromise with a cost for all three

The working lore supplies another useful connection: the conditioning suppresses a perceptual capability related to Ductus navigation. Fraying may expose that capability. This does not establish that every veteran is already a trained navigator.

Proposed use of that lore: a veteran transition programme, sponsored by the Guild, gives some former warriors a future outside campaigning. To make it credible the Guild must open training and selected navigation knowledge beyond its monopoly. The Janissariat accepts a smaller, less conditioned fighting institution; the Ascendant accepts a less unquestioning military foundation. The programme improves the prospects of peace without restoring the combat power that peace is costing.

This is a candidate negotiated settlement, not an automatic cure for fraying. Its immediate commitment of Guild capacity and its lasting access rights must be printed. The revelation about veterans' perceptions can remain something foresight discloses during play; the final bargain must be informed before anyone signs it.

Core rule to carry into the next draft

A round without qualifying combat reduces Readiness. A supplied convoy, Rest or routine Escort cannot cancel that decline. Readiness changes from combat must be resolved separately from civilian supply effects.

Print which operations count as actual combat. An order to fight is not sufficient if the target evades it or the force never reaches the engagement. No facilitator judgement is needed about whether players were “aggressive enough”.

The original setting also permits internal trials. They may offer a fourth route later, but their cost must be explicit. Do not add them as a free substitute that restores the old universal cooperation solution. Their precise cost is not established by this amendment.

Keep loyalty and obedience distinct

The Ascendant's military support should follow the condition that sustains the Janissariat, rather than being guaranteed by the title on a constitutional card. Declining conditioning creates uncertainty about future support. It does not automatically turn every soldier into an enemy, nor oblige the Janissariat players to betray the Ascendant.

Use the Readiness track and a printed consequence for the Ascendant rather than adding a second numerical loyalty track. The final implementation needs an explicit command or enforcement capability that becomes weaker when Readiness falls. A changed ending paragraph alone will not make this dependency felt during play.

An unauthorised campaign can preserve the Ascendant's military foundation while damaging the future they want. That is a more interesting disagreement than assuming all independent military action is opposition to the throne.

Unilateral action must remain possible

Give a faction's own players authority to commit its assets to a coercive action. The other faction and the Ascendant do not countersign that decision. The constitutional distinction is whether the act is lawful, and what consequences follow; it must not make a physical attempt impossible.

The Guild likewise needs a useful independent choice: withhold military passage or divert transport to its own priorities. It protects navigators or preserves control, but may leave the throne without a reliable army. An Ascendant choosing coercion must depend on actual military support and committed assets, rather than creating resources by decree.

Risk and reward test

For every unilateral action, print a reachable favourable result, a reachable unfavourable result and the condition separating them. Another player's decision, the selected target or a revealed circumstance can supply the uncertainty. Dice and a strategic map are unnecessary.

At least one aggressive choice must sometimes be preferable to accepting the offered bargain. Other players may accommodate it, counter it or offer a better bargain. They cannot simply refuse to let the choice be submitted.

Make foresight about these choices

Replace the round-two Navigation card's gap at Route 2 with branches tied to decisions that remain available. The Ascendant should learn the consequences of giving its army a war, trying to restrain it, or accepting a military operation whose outcome is uncertain.

Candidate vision — The quiet barracks
“If the army stands down, the settlements can recover. The conditioning will fray, and commanders will begin asking what authority your throne has over them. If you send them through Vethis to fight, they will return ready to serve you. The route and the people who supplied the passage will bear the cost.”

This is draft fiction, not yet a resolution card. Its final printed form must name the qualifying order, exact Readiness change, exact cost to the route or communities, and the Ascendant capability affected. Both consequences must be fixed before play. A player may know them and still disagree about which is preferable.

The two vision choices should reveal different dependencies: Navigation can show whether a military passage reaches a real engagement and what it costs the corridor; Security can show how a proposed restraint or campaign changes conditioning and the throne's support. Neither vision reveals what another player will choose.

What the Charter now settles

The military article must answer how an institution that needs fighting will be governed. Military autonomy permits it to seek campaigns and makes restraint difficult. Imperial command gives the Ascendant responsibility for keeping it active. Joint authorisation gives others a voice, with a real risk that delay lets conditioning fray. Remove the implication that joint command simply provides cheaper escorts with no comparable drawback.

The Navigation article decides who must carry that policy out and who may refuse. A transport obligation, a veto over targets and a limit on requisition become things worth bargaining over because the previous round has shown their consequences.

Checks before another simulation

  1. Two peaceful rounds must weaken conditioned military power even with abundant food.
  2. A cooperative limited campaign must require a meaningful concession from the Guild, the Janissariat and the Ascendant.
  3. A unilateral operation must be legal to submit without outsiders' approval, with both a favourable and an unfavourable reachable outcome.
  4. The Ascendant must face a practical loss of military support or enforcement capacity when conditioning declines.
  5. Neither routine escort nor internal trials may become an unpriced way to avoid the central tradeoff.
  6. The selected second-round vision must reveal at least one consequence relevant to the current decisions, including when Route is 2.
  7. The next report must separate successful survival from preservation of every faction's preferred institution.
